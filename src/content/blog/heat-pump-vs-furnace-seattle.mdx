For years, the answer to "heat pump or furnace" in Seattle was straightforward: furnace, because older heat pumps struggled once temperatures dropped near freezing. Modern cold-climate heat pumps have changed that calculation considerably.

## Where heat pumps win

A heat pump moves heat rather than generating it, which makes it dramatically more efficient than electric resistance heat and, in many cases, cheaper to run than gas over a full year - especially since it also provides cooling in the summer from the same system. For homes without existing ductwork, ductless mini-split heat pumps add cooling capability that a furnace-only setup never had.

## Where furnaces still win

Gas furnaces produce hotter air at the vent, which some homeowners simply prefer on the coldest days. They're also generally less expensive to install if you already have gas service and ductwork in place, and repair parts tend to be less costly than heat pump components.

## The real Seattle answer: it depends on your home

- **No existing ductwork or want cooling too?** A heat pump - likely ductless - is usually the better fit.
- **Already have gas service and just need a furnace replacement?** A furnace remains a solid, cost-effective choice, especially if summer cooling isn't a priority.
- **Want the best of both?** Dual-fuel systems pair a heat pump with a gas furnace backup, using whichever is more efficient at the current outdoor temperature.
