Boilers get a lot of misconceptions thrown at them. Here are six facts that might change how you think about the one quietly heating your home.

## 1. Boilers Aren't Actually Trying to Boil Water

Despite the name, a boiler only needs to heat water to around 60–70 degrees for effective radiant heating. Pushing it hotter than that just burns extra energy and drives up your utility bill for no real benefit.

## 2. Boilers Don't Humidify Your Air

A common myth is that boiler heat makes a home damp. In reality, the air stays dry and warm, with no danger of water condensation, peeling wallpaper, or mold caused by the system itself.

## 3. Water Pressure Matters More Than You'd Think

Condenser boilers need a minimum of 15 psi of water pressure to run properly. If your home's pressure falls short, you may need to upgrade the piping connecting to the municipal water main before the boiler can perform as designed.

## 4. Modern Units Are Genuinely Eco-Friendly

Today's condenser boilers recycle around 90% of the water and energy they use, while keeping heat and combustion byproducts from escaping into your living space — a real improvement over older oil furnaces in terms of indoor air quality.

## 5. Lowering Your Thermostat Pays Off

Depending on your home's insulation and your comfort settings, keeping the thermostat lower when you can can reduce energy bills by up to 90%.

## 6. Operation Is Simpler Than People Expect

Modern boilers ignite with the press of a button, heating water that circulates through radiant pipes throughout the home — no complicated startup required.
