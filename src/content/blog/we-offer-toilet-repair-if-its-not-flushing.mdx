A toilet that isn't flushing right is one of the most common plumbing complaints we hear, and the good news is that most causes are pretty easy to diagnose once you know what to look for.

## Not Flushing Completely

A toilet should clear fully on the first flush. If it isn't, the likely culprits are a clogged flush jet - the small opening that helps push water around the bowl - or a blockage further down in the toilet or main drain line.

## Nothing Happens When You Press the Handle

If pressing the handle produces no response at all, check the chain connecting the handle to the flapper - it may have come loose or disconnected. It's also worth confirming the water supply valve behind the toilet hasn't been shut off, which would prevent the tank from refilling.

## Toilet Keeps Running After Flushing

A toilet that continues to run well after a flush has finished usually has a defective flapper or a chain that isn't seating properly, allowing water to keep trickling from tank to bowl.

## Toilet Flushes Itself

If your toilet seems to flush on its own occasionally, a leaky flapper is almost always the cause - it lets water slowly escape from the tank until the tank refills and mimics the sound of a flush. Besides being unsettling, this wastes water and drives up your bill.

## Continuous Running Water Sound

A steady sound of running water that never quite stops usually points to a malfunctioning fill valve that can't shut off properly once the tank refills, sometimes overflowing into the bowl through the overflow tube.
