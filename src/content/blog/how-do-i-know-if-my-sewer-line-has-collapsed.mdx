A single slow drain is usually just that drain. A sewer line collapse tends to announce itself differently - across multiple fixtures, at the same time, in ways a plunger won't fix.

## Signs that point to the main line, not a single drain

- **Multiple drains backing up at once** - if your tub gurgles when you flush the toilet, or the washing machine drain backs up into a floor drain, that's a main line symptom.
- **Backups at the lowest fixture first** - basement floor drains and ground-floor toilets are usually the first to show trouble because they're the low point in the system.
- **Sewage odor in the yard** - especially near where the lateral line runs toward the street or septic tank.
- **A sudden low spot or sinkhole in the lawn** - this can indicate the pipe has fully given way underground and soil is settling into the void.
- **Unusually green, lush patches of grass** - sewage acts as fertilizer, so a suspiciously vibrant strip of lawn along the line's path is worth investigating.

## What a clog looks like instead

A single clogged drain typically affects only the fixtures downstream of the blockage, comes on gradually, and often responds - at least temporarily - to cabling. If cabling only buys you a few days before the same drain backs up again, that's a sign the real problem is further down the line.

## The only way to know for sure

Guessing based on symptoms alone risks either an unnecessary excavation or ignoring a real collapse until it gets worse. A [sewer camera inspection](/drain-camera-scoping/) shows the exact condition and location of any damage, which is also what determines whether [trenchless repair](/trenchless-sewer-line-repair-replacement/) is an option or a traditional dig is required.

If you're seeing more than one of these signs at once, it's worth scheduling an inspection before a slow problem becomes an emergency one.
