It happens to everyone eventually — a ring, an earring, a small toy slips out of your hand and disappears down the drain. Here's what to do before you panic.

## Act Fast, But Don't Run Water

The first move is to turn off the water supply immediately. Running water only pushes the item further into the pipes, so shut it off before you do anything else. Then take a look at the drain opening to see if the object is still visible and within reach.

## DIY Retrieval Methods

**Plunger method** — A cup plunger can create enough suction to dislodge an item, though this works best in straight pipe runs rather than older plumbing with lots of bends.

**Magnetic tool** — If the item is metal, a magnetic retrieval wand, available cheaply at most hardware stores, can pull it right up.

**Wire hanger** — A straightened coat hanger with a small hook bent into the end can carefully fish out an object without scratching the inside of the pipe.

**Wet-dry vacuum** — As a last resort, a wet-dry vac can suction small items out of a drain. You can rent one from Home Depot or Lowe's if you don't own one.

## Check the P-Trap

The U-shaped pipe under most sinks — the P-trap — is where small items often end up settling. You can unscrew the slip nuts, remove the trap, and check inside. Just be careful reassembling it correctly so you don't end up with a leak on top of your original problem.
