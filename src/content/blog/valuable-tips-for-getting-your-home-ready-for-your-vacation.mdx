Packing for a trip is the easy part — making sure your home is ready to be left alone for a while is what actually prevents an unpleasant surprise when you get back. Before you head out for an extended vacation, run through this quick checklist.

## Check for Leaks Before You Go

A small drip left unattended for a week or two can turn into real water damage. Walk through and check under sinks, around toilets, and near your water heater for any sign of moisture before you leave, and consider having a plumber take a look if anything's been slow to drain or seemed off lately.

## Set Your Thermostat, Don't Turn It Off

It's tempting to shut your HVAC system off entirely to save energy while you're away, but in Seattle's damp climate, turning off heat entirely can invite humidity and even frozen pipes in winter. Set your thermostat a few degrees warmer or cooler than usual instead of powering the system down completely.

## Consider Shutting Off Your Main Water Valve

For longer trips, shutting off the main water supply to your home eliminates the risk of a burst pipe or failed supply line causing damage while no one's there to notice. Just remember to drain remaining pressure from your faucets afterward.

## Have Your Water Heater and Sump Pump Checked

A water heater or [sump pump](/sump-pumps/) failure is one of the more common causes of vacation-time water damage. If either is due for a checkup, schedule it before you leave rather than after you get back.

## Don't Forget the Electrical Basics

Unplug small appliances that don't need to stay on, and consider a timer for a few lights to keep your home looking lived-in.
