A shower drain that clogs again and again isn't just bad luck - it's usually a combination of a few predictable culprits building up over time. Here's what tends to cause it and how to keep it from coming back.

## The Usual Culprits

**Hair buildup** is the most obvious cause. Shed strands accumulate in the drain and gradually form a substantial blockage, and in areas with hard water, minerals help those clumps solidify even further. **Soap residue** is a close second - minerals in the water react with soap products to create a sticky film that slowly narrows the inside of the pipe. **Mineral deposits** like calcium and lime also accumulate on their own over time, restricting flow even without hair or soap involved. And occasionally, **foreign objects** like a dropped soap bar or a small toy wedge into the drain and catch everything else that follows.

## Simple Prevention Steps

A monthly maintenance routine using a drain cleaner with baking soda can help keep buildup from taking hold. Installing an inexpensive drain guard or strainer catches hair before it ever reaches the pipe, which prevents the single biggest cause of shower clogs. A homemade combination of baking soda, vinegar, and lemon juice can also help break down light residue between deeper cleanings. And it goes without saying, dispose of hair and soap residue directly rather than letting it wash down the drain whenever you can.

## When It's Time to Call a Plumber

If you're dealing with multiple drains gurgling at once, water backing up into the tub during a shower, a persistent foul odor, or a clog that returns almost immediately after clearing it, the problem has likely moved beyond what a drain guard or home remedy can fix. In those cases, [hydro jetting](/hydrojetting/) uses pressurized water to blast through stubborn blockages far more thoroughly than a plunger or store-bought cleaner, and a camera inspection can reveal any underlying pipe issues, which are common in older Seattle-area homes.
