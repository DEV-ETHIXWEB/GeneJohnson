Choosing a heating system isn't a one-size-fits-all decision. Climate, home size, and location all factor into which technology makes the most sense — here's a look at the major options on the market today.

## Solar Heating

Solar heating systems draw warm air through filters and distribute it through the home, either using it immediately or storing it for later. It's an affordable and efficient option, especially in sunnier regions, though it depends on the amount of usable sun exposure your property gets.

## Geothermal Heating

Geothermal systems pull heat from the earth itself and can last more than fifty years — a lifespan few other systems can match. In winter they transfer that warmth indoors; in summer the process reverses to help cool the home.

## Heat Pumps

[Heat pumps](/heat-pump-services/) don't generate heat — they move it, pulling warmth from outside air even in cold weather and pushing it into your home. That makes them a cost-efficient, electric-powered option that handles both heating and cooling from a single system.

## Oil Heating

Oil heating systems ignite fuel in a combustion chamber and use a heat exchanger to warm air that then circulates through your ductwork. It remains a reliable option in areas without easy access to natural gas.

## Gas Heating

Natural gas heating typically costs about half of what electric heating does to run, and many utility providers offer rebates for gas furnace installations, which can help offset upfront costs.

## Electric Heating

Electric systems work best in milder climates and require relatively little maintenance, while still offering both heating and cooling capability in some configurations.

## Finding the Right Fit
