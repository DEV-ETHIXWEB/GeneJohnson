Seattle may be famous for its rain, but summer water demand still adds up, and more homeowners are looking for ways to conserve. Greywater reuse is one of the most practical options, turning water you'd otherwise send down the drain into a resource for your landscaping.

## What Counts as Greywater

Greywater is relatively clean wastewater from everyday activities like showers, bathroom sinks, washing machines, and tubs. It's distinct from blackwater, which comes from toilets and requires full treatment. Because greywater is comparatively clean, it can be safely diverted for landscape irrigation with the right system in place.

## Why It Makes Sense Here

Reusing greywater reduces how much treated municipal water your household depends on for non-potable uses, eases the burden on local sewer infrastructure, and lines up well with the environmental values many Seattle homeowners already hold.

## Common System Types

**Laundry-to-landscape** systems divert water directly from the washing machine through flexible tubing — one of the simplest and most affordable options. **Gravity-fed** systems rely on the natural slope of a property and basic filtration, with no pump required. **Pump systems** work well on the sloped lots common throughout the Seattle area. **Constructed wetlands** use plants and soil to provide natural treatment for larger-scale reuse.

## What You Gain

A greywater system reduces demand on municipal water, lowers your utility bills, provides nutrients that support healthy plant growth, decreases the energy used in water treatment, and gives you more flexibility to keep landscaping alive during dry spells.

## Doing It Safely and Legally

Untreated greywater should only be used for subsurface irrigation — it should never spray or pool where people or pets could contact it directly. Seattle has specific building codes governing greywater systems, so it's worth confirming requirements before installation. Stick to biodegradable, plant-safe soaps in your home, and avoid bleach and harsh chemicals that could harm your soil or plants.
