Most kitchen clogs don't happen overnight - they build up meal by meal from habits that seem harmless in the moment. Knowing which foods are the worst offenders is the first step to keeping your [drains](/drains/) flowing freely.

## The Biggest Culprits

**Grease, oils, and fats** top the list. They may go down the drain as a liquid, but they solidify as they cool inside your pipes, trapping other debris and slowly narrowing the passage until a full blockage forms.

**Coffee grounds** don't dissolve in water. Instead, they settle in the bends of your pipes and combine with grease or soap residue to form dense, stubborn obstructions - a real risk in any household with a daily coffee habit.

**Eggshells** create a gritty paste when they pass through a garbage disposal, and the membranes tangle with other debris, gradually worsening any existing blockage.

**Starchy foods** like rice, pasta, potatoes, and beans absorb water and expand once they're in the pipe, forming thick, sticky masses that resist flushing.

**Fibrous vegetables** - think celery, corn husks, and onion skins - wrap around disposal blades, forcing the motor to work harder and eventually forming rope-like clogs further down the line.

## Habits Worth Breaking

A few everyday habits make things worse: rinsing plates with large food scraps still attached, pouring cooking oil down the sink, leaning on the garbage disposal to handle everything, and ignoring slow drainage until it becomes a full stoppage.

## Keeping Your Kitchen Pipes Clear

Run hot water down the drain after each use to help move residue along. Install a quality drain strainer to catch scraps before they enter the pipe. Periodic enzymatic cleaning helps break down organic buildup without harming your plumbing, and it's worth avoiding harsh chemical cleaners, which can do more damage to pipes than the clog itself.
