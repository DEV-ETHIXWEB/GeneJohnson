Reaching for a jug of harsh chemical cleaner isn't the only option when a drain starts running slow. A handful of natural methods can clear mild clogs just as effectively, without the risk to your pipes, your septic system, or the environment.

## Salt and Hot Water

Coarse salt's gritty texture helps scour the inside of your pipes, while hot water melts away grease and soap buildup as it flows through. This combination works especially well for mild clogs in bathroom sinks and kitchen drains.

## Plain Boiling Water

Sometimes the simplest fix is the best one. Boiling water alone can dissolve grease and residue that has hardened as it cooled — it works best on kitchen sinks, where grease is the most common cause of slow drainage.

## Enzyme-Based Cleaners

Enzyme cleaners use natural bacteria to break down organic matter like hair and grease over time. They're safe for septic systems and work gently, without the corrosive effects that chemical cleaners have on aging pipes.

## Cream of Tartar and Salt

Mixing equal parts cream of tartar and salt with hot water creates a mild, effective cleaning solution that's particularly well suited to older plumbing systems common in Seattle's established neighborhoods.

## Bio-Based Drain Sticks

These dissolvable sticks release natural enzymes slowly over time, working preventatively to reduce buildup and odors between deeper cleanings.

## Prevention Habits Worth Keeping

Install drain screens in every sink and tub, avoid pouring grease or cooking oil down the drain, run a hot water rinse after washing dishes, and treat your pipes monthly with an enzyme-based cleaner to keep buildup from forming in the first place.

## When Natural Methods Aren't Enough

Natural solutions handle everyday buildup well, but they won't fix a clog caused by tree roots, a collapsed section of pipe, or years of accumulated scale. If a drain keeps backing up, smells persistently foul, or several drains in the house are slow at once, it's time to bring in a professional.
