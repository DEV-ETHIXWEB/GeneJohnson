Buying a house is one of the biggest decisions you'll make, and the electrical system is one of the easiest things to overlook during a walkthrough. A dedicated [electrical safety inspection](/electrical-safety-inspections/) by a certified electrician gives you a clear picture of what's really behind the walls before you sign anything.

## What an Inspection Actually Covers

A proper pre-purchase inspection means having a certified electrician check the home's entire electrical setup from top to bottom - the main panel, wiring, outlets, and switches. The goal is to catch hazards and code violations, including outdated systems like knob-and-tube wiring that carry real fire risk in older Seattle-area homes.

The process typically includes a visual inspection of the panel for damage or code violations, opening outlets and junction boxes to assess wiring condition, testing components for proper function, and calculating the home's electrical load capacity to confirm it can support your household's needs.

## Why It's Worth the Cost

**Family safety.** Outdated wiring, overloaded circuits, and improper grounding are hazards that aren't visible to the untrained eye. An inspection surfaces them before they become emergencies.

**Financial protection.** Catching problems early means you can negotiate repairs into the sale price instead of paying for them out of pocket after closing.

**Insurance requirements.** Many insurers require an inspection on older homes, and passing one helps you secure reasonable coverage rates.

**Capacity for modern living.** Inspectors can tell you whether the existing system can actually handle today's appliances, EV chargers, and smart home devices - or whether a [panel upgrade](/panel-replacements-upgrades/) is in your near future.

**Fire prevention.** Deteriorated insulation and overloaded circuits are leading causes of residential electrical fires. A trained eye catches these before move-in day.

**Hidden hazards.** Missing GFCI protection in kitchens and bathrooms, or exposed wiring in a basement or attic, are common findings that a casual walkthrough will never reveal.
