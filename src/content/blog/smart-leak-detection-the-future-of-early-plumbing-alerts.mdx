A small pinhole leak in a pipe can drip for weeks before it ever becomes obvious - long enough to warp flooring, feed mold growth, and weaken drywall behind the scenes. Smart leak detection technology exists specifically to close that gap between when a leak starts and when someone notices.

## How the technology works

Smart leak detectors are small, wireless sensors placed in areas most prone to leaks - under sinks, near water heaters, beside appliances like washing machines and dishwashers. When moisture is detected, the system sends an alert straight to your phone, and some higher-end setups can automatically trigger a shut-off valve to stop the flow before it causes further damage.

## Why it's worth considering

**Cost savings.** Catching a leak within hours instead of weeks is the difference between a quick repair and a full remediation project.

**Health protection.** Stopping water intrusion early keeps mold from ever getting the foothold it needs to spread.

**Possible insurance benefits.** Some insurers offer discounts for homes with monitored leak detection installed.

**Peace of mind.** Especially useful for vacation homes, rental properties, or anywhere you're not checking in person every day.

## Where to install sensors

The highest-value spots are kitchen and bathroom sinks, around the water heater, behind washing machines and dishwashers, near basement drains, and in crawl spaces - anywhere water damage would otherwise go unnoticed the longest.

## Detection isn't the same as a fix

Smart sensors are excellent at telling you something's wrong, but they don't repair anything themselves. Once you get an alert, you still need a licensed plumber to diagnose the source and handle the actual repair or [repiping](/repiping/) work if needed.

## Where this technology is headed

Expect smart leak detection to keep integrating more tightly with broader smart home systems, and eventually to help automate things like scheduling a technician or even starting an insurance claim the moment a leak is detected.
