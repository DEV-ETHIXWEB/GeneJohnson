Western Washington's windstorm season regularly knocks out power for hours - sometimes days - across the region, often concentrated in older, tree-lined neighborhoods where overhead lines take the brunt of it.

## The baseline: know your panel

Before anything else, know where your main electrical panel is and how to safely operate it. If you don't already have a standby generator, never attempt to power circuits by backfeeding through an outlet - this is illegal, dangerous to utility workers, and a common cause of injury during outages.

## Portable generators: what to know

Portable generators need a properly installed transfer switch to safely power circuits in your home - never a direct extension cord setup. They also require careful placement outdoors, well away from windows and doors, due to carbon monoxide risk.

## Standby generators: the set-and-forget option

A [whole-home standby generator](/whole-home-automatic-standby-generators/) sits outside like an AC condenser, runs on natural gas or propane, and starts automatically within seconds of detecting an outage - no manual fueling, no extension cords, and no need to be home when it happens. It's sized either to run your entire home or a curated set of essential circuits (heat, refrigeration, well pump, sump pump), depending on budget and available fuel supply.

## A short outage-prep checklist

- Keep a flashlight and battery bank charged and accessible - not buried in a closet
- Know your sump pump's battery backup status if you have one
- Keep a few days of water and non-perishable food on hand during storm season
- If you rely on medical equipment, prioritize a standby generator over portable options

Storm season is also the worst time to discover a generator problem - if you already have one, a pre-season maintenance check is worth scheduling before the first major windstorm rolls through.
