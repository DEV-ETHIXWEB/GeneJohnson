Seattle's mature trees are one of the best things about the neighborhood — until their roots find their way into your sewer line. Root intrusion is one of the most common causes of recurring drain problems we see, and it's a slow-building issue that often goes unnoticed until it's fairly advanced.

## How Roots Get In

Tree roots are drawn to moisture, and an underground sewer line is a reliable, constant source of it. Roots can work their way into even tiny cracks or loose joints in a pipe. Once inside, fine root hairs gradually turn into thick tangles that can clog a pipe completely. This process often takes years, which is why homeowners are frequently surprised by how bad it's gotten by the time they notice.

## Warning Signs to Watch For

A few patterns tend to show up before a full blockage:

- Clogs happening in multiple drains around the house, not just one
- Gurgling sounds coming from toilets or sinks
- Sewage odors near drains or in certain areas of the yard
- Wet patches or small sinkholes forming in the lawn
- Backups that get noticeably worse after heavy rain

## Rooter Service: A Temporary Fix

A rooter machine uses a rotating cable with blades to cut through roots blocking the pipe. It provides real relief, but unless the underlying pipe damage is repaired, the roots will grow right back into the same opening.

## Hydro Jetting: A More Thorough Clean

[Hydro jetting](/hydrojetting/) uses high-pressure water to blast roots and buildup out of the pipe more completely than a rooter machine can, which removes more of the debris that encourages root regrowth in the first place.

## Repair or Replacement for a Permanent Fix

For a lasting solution, options include pipe lining, which applies a root-resistant resin coating inside the existing pipe, or pipe bursting, which pulls a new pipe through the old one. A camera inspection is the best way to confirm the issue is fully resolved.

## Preventing Root Intrusion Going Forward

Schedule drain cleaning every one to two years, avoid planting new trees near your sewer line, and consider replacing aging clay pipes with modern PVC, which resists root intrusion far better.
