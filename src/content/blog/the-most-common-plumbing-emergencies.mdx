Some plumbing problems can wait for a scheduled appointment. Others can't. Here are four of the most common household plumbing emergencies we respond to, and why each one deserves prompt attention.

## No Hot Water

Local winters can get quite chilly, which makes losing hot water especially unwelcome. Annual maintenance is the best prevention, but even a well-maintained [water heater](/water-heaters/) can fail unexpectedly. When it does, it's usually not something that can wait until next week.

## A Broken Sump Pump

For homes with a basement or crawl space in a rainy region, a working sump pump isn't optional — it's the thing standing between a heavy storm and a flooded lower level. Pumps can become overworked, wear out, or malfunction over time, and the worst moment for that to happen is exactly when you need it most: during a major storm.

## A Broken Water Line

The main water line delivers water from the municipal supply into your home's plumbing. A break or leak here can cause a significant drop in water pressure and visible wet patches in the yard, and it often drives your water bill up dramatically in the process.

## A Clogged Toilet

Simple as it sounds, a clogged toilet is one of the most common household plumbing emergencies there is — and it can escalate quickly to an overflow that floods the bathroom. If plunging doesn't resolve it right away, it's better to stop using the fixture and call a professional than to attempt more aggressive fixes yourself.
