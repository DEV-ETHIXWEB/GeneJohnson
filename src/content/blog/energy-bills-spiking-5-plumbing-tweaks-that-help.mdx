When your energy bill jumps, the furnace usually gets blamed first - but your plumbing system, especially your water heater, is often just as responsible. Heating water is one of the largest energy expenses in a typical household, and small plumbing inefficiencies compound fast. Here are five fixes worth checking before you assume the bill is just going to stay high.

## 1. Track down hot-water leaks

A leaking hot water line costs you twice - once for the wasted water, again for the energy spent heating it. Check under sinks, around the water heater, and listen for the sound of water running when nothing's turned on.

## 2. Turn your water heater down to 120°F

Many water heaters ship set higher than they need to be. Dialing back to around 120°F is usually plenty for comfort while noticeably cutting the standby heating cost of keeping a full tank hot around the clock.

## 3. Insulate your hot water pipes

Pipes running through unconditioned spaces - garages, crawlspaces, unfinished basements - lose heat before the water ever reaches your tap. Insulating them keeps that heat where it belongs and gets hot water to you faster, which also means less water wasted waiting for it to warm up.

## 4. Flush sediment out of the tank

Mineral buildup at the bottom of a [water heater](/water-heaters/) tank forces it to work harder to heat the same amount of water, and it's often the reason for popping or rumbling noises. A professional flush restores efficiency and extends the unit's life.

## 5. Upgrade old fixtures

Modern low-flow showerheads and faucet aerators use meaningfully less hot water per minute without a noticeable drop in pressure - one of the easiest upgrades to make back in savings.

## Worth checking too

Beyond these five, keep an eye on water pressure, fix any running toilets promptly, and don't rule out a [slab leak](/slab-leak-repair/) if your bill is climbing but you can't find an obvious cause.
