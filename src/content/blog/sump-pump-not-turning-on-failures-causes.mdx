Nothing is more unsettling than heading down to the basement during a heavy storm and finding your [sump pump](/sump-pumps/) sitting silent while water rises in the pit. Sump pumps are simple machines, but there are several specific points of failure that can leave them dead exactly when you need them most. Here's what usually goes wrong.

## Power Problems

A surprising number of "dead pump" calls turn out to be power issues rather than pump issues. A tripped GFCI outlet, a tripped breaker, or a loose plug connection can cut power to the pump without any obvious sign. Damaged cords and worn outlets can also interrupt the circuit intermittently, which makes the problem hard to catch until the pump is needed.

## A Stuck or Failed Float Switch

The float switch is what tells the pump to turn on as water rises in the pit. If it gets wedged against the pit wall, tangles in its own cord, or collects debris, it may never trigger the pump at all. Worn electrical contacts inside the switch can cause the same failure even when the float itself is moving freely.

## A Clogged Pump

Silt and sludge build up in sump pits over time, and that debris can jam the impeller, the spinning component that actually moves water. A clogged pump often hums without moving water, or discharges only a weak trickle instead of a strong stream.

## A Failed Check Valve

The check valve keeps water from flowing back down into the pit once it's been pumped out. When this valve fails, water rushes back in immediately, forcing the pump to restart over and over or simply overwhelming it entirely.

## Blocked Discharge Lines

Frozen pipes, yard debris, or a poorly routed discharge line can all restrict where the water is supposed to go. Undersized discharge piping adds extra back pressure on the pump as well, which shortens its working life even when everything else is functioning correctly.

## The Pump Has Simply Reached End of Life

Most sump pumps last somewhere between 7 and 10 years. Excessive noise, frequent cycling, or a pump that seems to be struggling are all signs it's nearing the end, even if it hasn't failed outright yet.

## Don't Skip a Battery Backup

Since sump pumps are needed most during storms, and storms are exactly when power outages happen, a battery backup system is one of the best investments a homeowner can make. It keeps your basement protected even when the grid goes down.
