Spring cleaning shouldn't stop at the closets and windows — it's also the perfect time to give your plumbing a once-over before summer guests and higher water use arrive. Here's a room-by-room checklist.

## Kitchen and Bathroom

- Check every faucet for leaks and drips; even a small drip wastes real money over a year.
- Install strainers on all drains to catch hair and food debris before it becomes a clog.
- Test toilets for hidden leaks by adding a few drops of food coloring to the tank. If the toilet is leaking, color will appear in the bowl within 30 minutes.
- Look for cracks in the tank and bowl, and make sure the toilet flushes properly — replace worn internal components if it doesn't.
- Exercise every shut-off valve so it doesn't seize up when you actually need it in an emergency.
- Soak mineral-clogged showerheads in vinegar overnight to restore full water flow.

## Appliances

- Set your [water heater](/water-heaters/) no higher than 120°F to prevent scalding and reduce energy use.
- Drain sediment from the tank periodically to keep it running efficiently, and plan to replace any unit older than 15 years.
- Keep flammable materials well away from water heaters and furnaces.
- Inspect the hoses on your dishwasher, washing machine, and ice maker for cracking or bulging — replace any hose that looks weak or is more than ten years old.
- Clean out the washing machine's lint trap.

## Throughout the House

- Run water down seldom-used drains to keep trap seals intact and sewer gas from creeping in.
- Check under sinks and in the basement for slow leaks.
- Test your [sump pump](/sump-pumps/) by pouring water into the basin and confirming it kicks on.
- Where sewer backup is a risk, a backflow valve on floor drains adds an extra layer of protection.
- Compare water meter readings before bed and first thing in the morning — a change with no water running points to a hidden leak.

## Outside

- Clear gutters, downspouts, and yard drains of winter debris.
- Check plumbing vents on the roof for bird nests.
- Turn on outdoor faucets and confirm they flow freely, without hissing or sputtering.
