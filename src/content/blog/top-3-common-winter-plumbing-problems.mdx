Winter reliably brings the same handful of plumbing calls every year. None of them are complicated to prevent, but all of them get expensive fast if they're ignored. Here are the three that come up most often, and what actually keeps them from happening.

## 1. Frozen Pipes

When temperatures drop below freezing, any pipe without adequate protection is at risk of cracking or bursting as the water inside expands — which usually means water damage and, if it isn't caught quickly, mold. Adding protective insulation to exposed pipes before the cold arrives, and leaving faucets dripping during a hard freeze, keeps water moving and lowers the risk substantially. If a pipe does burst, [emergency repair](/emergency-plumbing/) service can limit the damage.

## 2. Clogged Drains

Holiday gatherings mean more cooking, more dishes, and more strain on kitchen and bathroom drains than the rest of the year combined. Cooking oil, fat, and food scraps poured down the kitchen sink are a leading cause of holiday-season clogs, and shower drains take a hit too with more house guests using them. Installing hair traps in showers and keeping grease out of the sink entirely go a long way. If a clog does form, professional drain cleaning clears it faster and more thoroughly than most DIY methods.

## 3. Water Heater Strain

Cold incoming water combined with more house guests means your water heater has to work harder just to keep up with normal demand, let alone the extra showers and dishwashing loads that come with entertaining. An annual tank flush and inspection before winter helps it handle the load. Watch for early warning signs like a lack of hot water, inconsistent temperature, or water that takes noticeably longer to heat up — all of which suggest it's time for a checkup, not a full replacement, if caught early enough.

## The Common Thread

Every one of these problems is more manageable — and cheaper — the earlier it's caught. A little proactive maintenance before winter hits, and prompt attention to the first warning sign, is usually all it takes to avoid a full-blown plumbing emergency during the coldest stretch of the year.
