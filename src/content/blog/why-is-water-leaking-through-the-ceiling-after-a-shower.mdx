Finding a wet spot or stain on the ceiling below your bathroom after a shower is unsettling — and it should be, because it almost always means water is getting somewhere it shouldn't. Here are the most common causes we find, roughly in order of how often we see them.

## Sealant and grout failure

The water-tight seal along the edges of your shower plays a crucial role in keeping moisture contained. Cracked caulk or deteriorated grout between tiles lets water sneak behind the surface. Minor gaps can sometimes be re-caulked as a DIY fix, but widespread grout failure needs a professional re-grout.

## A leaking shower pan

Cracks or clogs in the pan beneath your shower floor require professional replacement, along with an inspection for any water damage that's already occurred underneath.

## Faulty faucets, valves, and liners

A dripping faucet or valve can slowly cause enough moisture buildup to reach the ceiling below. Likewise, an improperly installed shower liner won't create the watertight seals and overlaps it's supposed to, letting water escape behind the walls.

## Drain problems

A loose drain connection or a clog that causes water to back up and overflow can both send water where it doesn't belong. For stubborn clogs, professional [drain](/drains/) service is safer and more effective than chemical cleaners, which can damage pipes over time.

## Supply line and pipe leaks

Cracks in a water supply line inside the wall may require opening the wall to access and repair. Leaks from a shower head or pipe hidden behind the tile often need professional leak detection equipment to pinpoint.

## Cracked floor tiles

Damaged tile on the shower floor can let water through to the subfloor, typically requiring demolition, waterproofing, and a new pan.

## Don't wait to investigate

The longer a ceiling leak goes unaddressed, the more likely it is to cause mold and structural water damage. If you're seeing stains, discoloration, or dripping after showers, [contact us](/contact/) for a leak inspection before it becomes a bigger repair.
