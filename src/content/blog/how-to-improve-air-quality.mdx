Summer is hard on your indoor air. Between running the air conditioner nonstop, doors and windows opening and closing all day, and outdoor debris getting tracked inside, dust and allergens build up fast. Here's how to reset your home's air quality as the season turns.

## Replace Your Air Filters

The simplest, highest-impact step is a fresh filter. It gets rid of the dust and dirt that accumulated over the summer along with trapped pollen, and it can prolong the life of your air conditioner while cutting down on allergy symptoms circulating through the house.

## Skip the Harsh Cleaning Chemicals

Bleach, ammonia, and many commercial cleaning products do more than clean surfaces — they degrade indoor air quality too. A gentler alternative is a homemade cleaner using vinegar, baking soda, and orange oil, which handles most household messes without the fumes.

## Watch for Gas Leaks

Fall cleanup is also a good time to check for warning signs of a gas leak: the smell of rotten eggs or sulfur, houseplants dying without explanation, or a faint whistling sound near appliances. If you ever feel dizzy or faint indoors for no clear reason, treat it as an emergency and get professional help immediately.

## A Fresh Start for the Season

Combining a filter change with a broader look at your [air quality](/air-quality/) setup — and scheduling routine heating and cooling maintenance — sets your home up for a healthier fall and winter.
