Every toilet eventually reaches a point where repair no longer makes sense. The tricky part is knowing when you've crossed that line. Here are the signs we look for when deciding whether a [toilet](/toilet-repair-installation/) needs another fix or a full replacement.

## Constant Running and Frequent Repairs

An occasional repair is normal wear and tear. But when repairs become routine, it's often a sign the toilet itself is wearing out rather than any single part. Persistent refilling, repeated component failures, and a climbing water bill from constant running are all signs the whole unit is on its way out.

## Cracks, Leaks, and Water Damage

Water pooling around the base of the toilet is a warning sign that shouldn't be ignored. Even small cracks can worsen over time, and left unaddressed, they risk water damage to the subfloor beneath the bathroom.

## Age and Inefficiency

Toilets installed before the 1990s often predate modern efficiency standards. Older models can use anywhere from 3.5 to 7 gallons per flush, compared to about 1.28 gallons for a current model - a difference that adds up to thousands of wasted gallons a year.

## Recurring Clogs

If a toilet regularly needs multiple flushes to clear, that's usually a sign of an outdated or failing flush mechanism rather than a fluke. Modern flushing technology has largely solved this problem, and replacement often ends the frustration for good.

## Physical Instability

A toilet that rocks or shifts when you sit down has a mounting problem. Left alone, that movement can crack the base or the wax seal below it, leading to a hidden leak under the flooring.

## Remodeling or Selling Your Home

Even a functional older toilet can look dated. Updating it is a relatively small investment that improves both the room's appearance and how buyers perceive the bathroom overall.

## Performance Changes

Strange noises, a weak flush, or generally inconsistent performance usually point to internal wear that's beyond a simple repair.
