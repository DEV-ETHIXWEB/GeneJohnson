Those little plastic socket holes are like siren calls to curious infant fingers. Once a baby starts crawling, electrical outlets, cords, and power strips become some of the first things they'll try to investigate - and a little preparation goes a long way toward keeping them safe.

## Secure the outlets

There are a few good options depending on how much convenience you're willing to trade for security:

- **Tamper-resistant outlets** - Since 2008, the National Electrical Code has required these in new construction. They use spring-loaded shutters that only open when both slots are pressed at once, which blocks a single probing finger or object.
- **Outlet caps and covers** - Simple plastic inserts that plug the holes completely, though they need to be removed every time you actually use the outlet.
- **In-use covers** - Plastic boxes that sit over the outlet and let cords pass through safely, useful for outlets that stay occupied.

If your home doesn't have tamper-resistant outlets yet, our [electricians](/electrical-wiring-rewiring/) can swap them in during a routine visit.

## Manage the cords

Loose cables are just as tempting as outlets. Flat cord covers, velcro cord shorteners, gaffer's tape, and cable ties all help keep wires out of reach and off the floor. It's also worth minimizing how much you rely on extension cords - if you find yourself using them regularly, it's usually a sign you need more outlets installed rather than more cords run across the room.

## Power strips need attention too

Power strips are an underrated hazard in a home with a baby. Add plastic safety covers, keep cords bundled neatly, and where possible, consider having dedicated outlets installed instead of relying on a strip long-term.

## Don't forget when you travel

Grandma's house, a rental, a hotel room - none of them are childproofed the way your home is. Pack a couple of portable outlet covers and some cable management supplies for trips so you're not caught off guard somewhere unfamiliar.
