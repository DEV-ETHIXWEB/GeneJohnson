Replacing a water heater is one of those decisions homeowners are forced into on short notice - usually because the old one just failed. Knowing the tradeoffs ahead of time makes that decision a lot less stressful.

## Tank water heaters

A traditional tank heats and stores 40-80 gallons of water, keeping it hot around the clock. They're cheaper to buy and install, and repairs are typically simpler and less expensive. The tradeoff is standby heat loss - you're paying to keep water hot even when no one's using it - and a finite supply that can run out during back-to-back showers.

## Tankless water heaters

Tankless units heat water on demand as it flows through, with no storage tank and no standby loss. That means endless hot water and typically lower energy bills, especially in households with high hot-water usage. They also take up far less space, which matters in smaller mechanical closets or garages.

The tradeoffs: higher upfront equipment and installation cost, and some installs require a larger gas line or dedicated electrical circuit depending on the unit's capacity.

## A quick way to decide

- **Choose tank** if you're replacing on a budget, your gas line and space are already sized for it, and you don't need simultaneous high-volume hot water use.
- **Choose tankless** if you want to eliminate running out of hot water, plan to stay in the home long enough to recoup the difference in energy savings, or are short on installation space.
