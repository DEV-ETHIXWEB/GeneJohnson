Summer is when household water use really climbs. The average family typically uses about 320 gallons of water a day, but according to EPA data, that number can jump to anywhere from 1,000 to 3,000 gallons a day once lawns, pools, and extra laundry loads enter the picture. The good news: you don't have to give up summer fun to keep that number in check.

## Make Lawn Watering Part of the Fun

Watering the lawn doesn't have to be a chore that happens while the kids are inside. Turn on the sprinkler or hose and let everyone cool off while the yard gets what it needs — you're accomplishing two things with the water you were already going to use.

## Water Balloon Combat, Minus the Balloons

Swap balloons for saturated sponges. A sponge fight delivers the same cooling, splashing fun as a water balloon battle but uses a fraction of the water, and there's no cleanup of popped balloon scraps in the yard afterward.

## Teach a Gardening Habit

Getting kids involved in the garden does double duty — it teaches patience and follow-through, and it's a natural opening to talk about water conservation. Letting them water their own small plot with a watering can, rather than a running hose, reinforces the habit early.

## Head to the Public Pool

A trip to a community pool gives everyone a way to cool off without touching your home's water use at all, and it comes with professional supervision built in.

None of this means sacrificing a good summer. A few small swaps in how your family stays cool can meaningfully soften the water bill spike that comes every summer.
