A burst pipe can put hundreds of gallons of water into your home per hour. What you do in the first five minutes matters more than almost anything a plumber does after arriving.

## 1. Shut off your main water valve - immediately

This is the single most important step. Your main shutoff is typically near where the water line enters the house - a basement, crawlspace access, garage, or utility closet. If you don't already know where yours is, find it today, before you need it in an emergency.

## 2. Shut off electricity to the affected area if water is near outlets or panels

Water and electricity are a serious combination. If flooding is anywhere near outlets, panels, or plugged-in appliances, shut off power to that area at the breaker - don't stand in water to do it if there's any doubt about safety.

## 3. Call for emergency service

[Emergency plumbing](/emergency-plumbing/) calls get priority dispatch specifically because time determines the amount of damage. Have the location of the leak and your main shutoff status ready when you call - it helps us prioritize and prepare the right parts for the truck.

## 4. Start moving water and valuables

While waiting, move furniture, electronics, and belongings out of the affected area, and start removing standing water with towels, a wet/dry vacuum, or buckets if the volume allows it safely.

## 5. Document the damage

Photos and video of the leak and resulting damage, taken as soon as it's safe to do so, make insurance claims significantly smoother later.

## After the immediate crisis

Once the leak is stopped and repaired, watch for signs of hidden moisture over the following days - musty odors, discoloration on walls or ceilings, or peeling paint - since water often travels further than it initially appears to.
