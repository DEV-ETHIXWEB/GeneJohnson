A damaged sewer line used to mean one thing: heavy equipment tearing across your yard or driveway for days at a time. [Trenchless sewer line repair](/trenchless-sewer-line-repair-replacement/) changes that equation, fixing the problem underground through small access points instead of a full excavation.

## Two Trenchless Methods

**Pipe lining** inserts a resin-coated sleeve into the damaged pipe. Once in place, it hardens to form a durable new pipe inside the old one.

**Pipe bursting** takes a different approach, breaking apart the old pipe while simultaneously pulling a new one into its place.

Both methods avoid the need to dig a continuous trench along the length of the pipe.

## Signs Your Sewer Line May Be Damaged

Watch for multiple slow drains throughout the house, unpleasant sewer odors in the yard or home, an unusually lush or green patch of grass (a sign of sewage seepage feeding the soil), frequent backups in toilets, sinks, or tubs, and foundation cracks caused by soil erosion around a leaking line.

## Why Trenchless Repair Makes Sense

The advantages go well beyond convenience: minimal disruption to landscaping and hardscaping, faster completion - often days instead of weeks - durable materials built to last, costs that are comparable to traditional excavation once you factor in landscaping restoration, and a smaller environmental footprint overall.

## Is Your Property a Good Fit?

Trenchless repair works especially well when the damaged section runs beneath landscaping or a driveway, when the pipe has moderate to severe damage but hasn't fully collapsed, and when preserving the property matters as much as fixing the pipe. Local Seattle-area building codes also play a role in determining which method is permitted for a given property, so it's worth having a professional evaluate your specific situation.
