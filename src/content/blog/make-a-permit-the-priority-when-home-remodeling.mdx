It's tempting to skip the paperwork when you're excited to start a kitchen or bathroom remodel, but the permit is one step you don't want to shortcut, especially when plumbing is involved.

## Permits Keep the Work Safe

A permit isn't just a formality — it triggers periodic and final inspections, ensuring everything is safe and durable before the walls close back up. Without that objective review, homeowners risk hidden structural damage and safety hazards that don't show up until much later.

## Licensed Pros Prefer Permitted Work

Reputable, licensed contractors generally prefer to work with a permit in hand, because it holds the job to industry standards. That translates into longer-lasting, safer upgrades and fewer repairs down the road — which is exactly what you want from a [toilet repair or installation](/toilet-repair-installation/), a repipe, or any plumbing work tied into your remodel.

## The Cost of Skipping It

Unpermitted work has a way of surfacing at the worst possible time — during a home inspection or a sale. When it does, you can face fines and be required to redo the work to meet code, sometimes at double the original cost.

## When You Actually Need One

As a general rule, structural changes — moving walls, altering plumbing or sewer lines, electrical work, or HVAC changes — typically require a permit. Purely cosmetic updates usually don't, but it's always worth confirming with your local jurisdiction before you start.
