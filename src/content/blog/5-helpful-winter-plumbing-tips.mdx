Winter puts more strain on a home's plumbing than any other season — cold snaps threaten exposed pipes, and holiday hosting pushes drains and water heaters harder than usual. A little preparation before the cold really sets in can save you an emergency call once it does.

## 1. Shut Off Outdoor Faucets

Before temperatures drop, close your outdoor faucets and drain any remaining water from the lines. Water left sitting in an exterior line is one of the most common sources of winter freeze damage, and a burst outdoor spigot can send water into the house before anyone notices.

## 2. Insulate Exposed Pipes

Any pipe running through a basement, attic, or exterior wall is exposed to cold in a way that interior plumbing isn't. Foam pipe insulation is inexpensive and simple to install, and it makes a real difference during a hard freeze.

## 3. Know Where Your Main Shut-Off Valve Is

If a pipe does burst, knowing exactly where your main water shut-off valve is — typically near your water meter — can be the difference between a quick fix and a flooded basement. Find it now, not during an emergency.

## 4. Have Your Water Heater Inspected

Winter asks more of your water heater than any other season. Having a professional flush sediment from the tank and check its components beforehand helps ensure it can keep up when demand is highest.

## 5. Watch What Goes Down the Kitchen Drain

Holiday cooking and entertaining bring a spike in grease, oil, and food scraps heading toward the kitchen drain. Keep grease out of the sink entirely — let it cool and toss it in the trash instead — to avoid a clog right when you have the most guests in the house.

## Prevention Beats Emergency Repairs

None of these steps take long, but skipping them tends to show up later as an emergency call during the worst possible week to need one.
