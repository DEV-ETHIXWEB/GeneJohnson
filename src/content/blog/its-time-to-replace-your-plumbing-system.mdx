Repairing a plumbing fixture always looks cheaper in the moment, but that's not always true once you factor in the whole picture. Here's how to tell when replacement is actually the smarter move.

## Washing Machines

Leaks caused by cracks in the tub, or by loose drums or motor mounts, aren't cheap or easy to fix - and they tend to recur. Modern washing machines also offer meaningful water and energy savings, which can offset a good chunk of the upfront replacement cost over time.

## Shower Fixtures

Hard water leaves sediment inside shower components, which can trigger clogs and noticeably reduced water pressure. Leaks or a burst pipe behind the wall carry real damage risk. When a shower starts showing these signs, upgrading to a water-efficient model often pays for itself through lower water bills.

## Kitchen Sinks

A cracked sink is a slow leak waiting to happen, and the water damage that follows is rarely cheap. Replacement is often the most practical solution - and while you're at it, pairing the new sink with a water-efficient faucet is an easy upgrade to make at the same time.

## Water Heaters

Rust spots, active leaks, and unusual noise from sediment buildup are all signs your [water heater](/water-heaters/) is on its way out. If you're due for a replacement anyway, it's worth considering a [tankless water heater](/tankless-water-heaters/), which delivers a limitless supply of hot water without the storage tank a traditional unit requires.

## The Bigger Picture

Repairs will always look like the cheaper choice up front. But newer plumbing equipment tends to be more efficient and less maintenance-heavy, which means the math often favors replacement once you're facing repeat repairs.
