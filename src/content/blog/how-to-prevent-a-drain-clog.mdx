Drain clogs are one of the most common plumbing calls we get, and one of the most preventable. Older homes in particular tend to be more clog-prone, thanks to narrower pipe diameters and decades of accumulated buildup. The good news is that a small amount of routine maintenance heads off most clogs before they ever become a real problem.

## What Actually Causes Clogs

The usual suspects are hair, congealed grease and cooking oil, soap residue, food particles, and small objects that end up down a drain by accident. In older homes, there's an additional factor worth watching for: tree roots infiltrating underground sewer lines, which can cause slow drainage throughout the house rather than at a single fixture.

## Quick Habits That Prevent Most Clogs

- Install mesh drain screens in sinks, tubs, and showers to catch hair and debris before it enters the pipe
- Run a monthly baking soda and vinegar treatment to keep grease and residue from building up
- Never pour grease or cooking oil down the drain - let it cool and dispose of it in the trash instead
- Go easy on the garbage disposal with fibrous or starchy foods that don't break down cleanly
- Add hair catchers in the shower
- Flush kitchen drains weekly with hot water to help move grease along before it hardens

## Longer-Term Maintenance

Beyond the weekly and monthly habits, a few things are worth doing on a yearly basis: professional [drain cleaning](/drains/) to clear buildup that home remedies can't reach, routine garbage disposal maintenance, and a periodic inspection of underground plumbing in older homes to catch tree root intrusion before it causes a full blockage.
