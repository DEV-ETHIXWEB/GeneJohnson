As the weather cools and Seattle homes button up for the season, ventilation tends to get overlooked. But a tightly sealed house is only comfortable if air is actually moving the way it should. Before winter sets in, it's worth taking a closer look at three areas that quietly control how well your home breathes.

## Check your ducts

Dirty ducts, blockages, or excess moisture in your duct system can wreak havoc on ventilation throughout the house - pushing your [furnace](/furnace-repair-and-maintenance/) to work harder just to move air where it needs to go. A professional inspection during your fall maintenance visit is the easiest way to catch buildup or damage before it turns into an efficiency problem.

## Don't ignore the attic and crawlspace

Attics and crawlspaces are two of the most common spots for moisture issues to develop, and both play a bigger role in home comfort than most homeowners realize. Poor attic ventilation can lead to real problems heating and cooling your home, and many Seattle-area houses simply don't have adequate crawlspace ventilation to begin with. If it's been a while since either space was evaluated, it's worth having a technician take a look.

## Give your range hood some attention

With cooler weather comes more time in the kitchen, and that means your range hood's ventilation fan is working overtime. It vents smoke and cooking byproducts out of the home, which directly improves indoor air quality - but only if it's functioning properly. A quick check now saves you from cooking in a smoky kitchen all winter.

## Why this matters before winter

Once your home is sealed up tight against the cold, ventilation problems that were minor annoyances in summer can become real comfort issues. Addressing ducts, attic and crawlspace airflow, and range hood function now means a more comfortable, better-ventilated home when temperatures drop.
