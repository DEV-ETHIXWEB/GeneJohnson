If you live in the Seattle area, chances are your basement has seen its share of wet weather. A [sump pump](/sump-pumps/) is the quiet piece of equipment standing between a heavy rainstorm and a flooded basement — but only if it's actually working, and only if you have one in the first place. Here are four signs it's time to install or replace one.

## 1. Your Existing Pump Isn't Turning On

If it's rained hard and you've noticed puddles forming outside, you should hear your sump pump kick on. If it's silent when it should be working, that's a malfunction, not a coincidence. Pumps older than about 10 years commonly start failing, and at that age, replacement is usually more sensible than repair.

## 2. Water Is Pooling in Your Basement

Standing water or persistent dampness in the basement means one of two things: you don't have adequate sump pump protection, or the one you have has stopped doing its job. In a rainy climate like ours, that moisture isn't going to resolve itself — it will keep coming back with every storm until the drainage problem is addressed.

## 3. You Live Somewhere That Floods Easily

Geography matters here. Homes in wetter regions face meaningfully higher basement flooding risk than homes in arid climates. Around Seattle, going without a dependable sump pump simply isn't a great bet, even if your basement has stayed dry so far.

## 4. You're Noticing Mold or a Musty Smell

Even modest, chronic dampness is enough to get mold and mildew growing, along with the musty odor that comes with it. A functioning sump pump keeps the basement dry enough that fungal growth never gets a foothold in the first place.
