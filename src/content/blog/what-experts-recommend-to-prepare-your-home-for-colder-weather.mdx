Every year around this time, our phones start ringing more. Gene Johnson General Manager Kimberly Kean has seen the pattern for years: "We see an increase in calls for no heat, no hot water, and drainage issues as winter rains begin and as Washington sees multiple days in a row of low temperatures."

## Why Cold Snaps Cause Problems

It's rarely a single cold morning that causes trouble. The real risk shows up after several consecutive days of subfreezing temperatures, when pipes, water heaters, and furnaces that have been quietly struggling all year finally give out. Combine that with the start of Seattle's winter rains, and drainage systems that were already borderline get pushed over the edge.

## The Three Calls We Hear Most

- **No heat** — furnaces and heat pumps working overtime, or simply failing, once the temperature drops and stays down
- **No hot water** — water heaters that were limping along suddenly can't keep up with demand
- **Drainage issues** — combined with heavy rain, marginal drain lines back up faster

## Getting Ahead of It

The best time to have your furnace or [heat pump](/heat-pump-services/) checked is before that first real cold snap, not during it. The same goes for your water heater — a quick inspection now can catch a failing unit before it leaves you without hot water on the coldest week of the year. If it's been a while since your last maintenance visit, [schedule now](/schedule-now/) before the temperature drops and the wait times get longer.
