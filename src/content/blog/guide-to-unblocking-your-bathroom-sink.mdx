A slow or fully blocked bathroom sink is one of the most common calls we get, and one of the more fixable ones - if you know what you're dealing with.

## What's clogging it

**Hair and soap buildup** are public enemy number one in bathroom sinks. Together they form a sludgy mass that grabs onto everything else that goes down the drain.

**Product residue** from toothpaste, makeup, and lotion hardens over time and adds to that buildup layer by layer.

**Seattle's hard water** deposits minerals inside the pipe that gradually narrow the passage, making any future clog worse than it would otherwise be.

**Foreign objects** - bottle caps, jewelry, small toys - occasionally slip down the drain and become the nucleus a clog builds around.

## Five things to try before calling anyone

1. **Plunge it.** A cup plunger with a few vigorous pumps clears plenty of minor clogs on its own.
2. **Baking soda and vinegar.** The fizzy reaction creates a mild acid that can break down organic buildup - let it sit, then flush with hot water.
3. **Boiling water.** A careful flush can dislodge greasy buildup, though skip this step on older PVC that may not tolerate the heat.
4. **A drain snake.** Feeding a snake down the drain lets you physically pull out hair and debris a plunger can't reach.
5. **Clean the P-trap.** Disconnecting the U-shaped pipe under the sink and clearing it directly is often the most effective DIY fix, since that's where clogs frequently collect.

## When it's a job for a plumber

Recurring clogs, gurgling sounds, foul odors, or any sign of a leak under the sink mean the problem has outgrown DIY tools. We use hydro-jetters and video inspection cameras to clear buildup completely and see exactly what's happening inside the pipe - not just push the clog further down the line.

## Keeping it clear going forward

A simple drain strainer catches hair before it becomes a problem, a weekly hot water flush helps keep grease and residue moving, and an annual professional inspection is worth scheduling for older Seattle homes where pipe buildup tends to accumulate faster.
