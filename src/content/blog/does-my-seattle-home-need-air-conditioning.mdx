For most of the region's history, Western Washington's mild summers made air conditioning feel unnecessary. That's changed. Multi-day heat events well into the 90s and beyond have become a regular part of Puget Sound summers, and homes built for our historically cool climate often trap heat with nowhere to go.

## Why older homes struggle more

Homes built before AC was common were designed for heat retention, not heat rejection - smaller windows on the hot-side exposure, minimal attic ventilation in some cases, and no mechanical cooling of any kind. During a heat event, indoor temperatures in these homes can climb well above outdoor temperatures by late afternoon and stay there overnight.

## Your options if you don't have ductwork

This is the most common objection we hear, and it's a solved problem: [ductless mini-split heat pumps](/cooling/ductless-mini-split-heat-pumps/) provide room-by-room cooling (and heating) without any ductwork at all - just a small line set between an outdoor unit and one or more indoor heads. Many homeowners are surprised how unobtrusive the installation is.

## What it's actually worth

Beyond comfort, a functioning cooling system matters for health during extreme heat, particularly for children, older adults, and anyone with a respiratory or cardiovascular condition. It also tends to add resale value in a market where buyers increasingly ask about cooling during showings.

If you've been putting off AC because your home "was never built for it," a same-day estimate can usually tell you within an hour whether ducted or ductless makes more sense for your specific layout.
