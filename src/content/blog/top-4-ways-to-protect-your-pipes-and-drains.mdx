Pet owners deal with a few plumbing hazards that don't show up in most home maintenance guides. If you share your home with cats or dogs, here are four ways to protect your pipes and drains from pet-related damage.

## 1. Cover Drain Openings

Curious kittens can end up exploring floor drains and getting stuck, sometimes requiring a professional rescue. Exposed pipes are also at risk from puppies that like to chew, which can lead to damage serious enough to require replacement. Installing drain covers and keeping exposed pipes out of reach solves both problems at once.

## 2. Use a Pet-Specific Drain Strainer

Bathing a cat or dog can send a surprising amount of hair down the drain, and that hair is a common cause of clogs. A drain strainer designed specifically to catch pet hair, with tightly spaced holes, costs more than a standard strainer but is far cheaper than a plumber visit to clear a hair clog.

## 3. Never Flush Cat Litter

Even litter marketed as "flushable" can harden inside your pipes like concrete, creating a blockage that's difficult to clear. Used litter should always go in the trash. It's also worth discouraging dogs from drinking out of the toilet — shed hair from a curious dog can contribute to clogs of its own.

## 4. Stay Proactive

The common thread across all of this is prevention. A little extra attention to your [drains](/drains/) goes a long way toward avoiding an unnecessary plumbing emergency caused by a pet-related surprise.
