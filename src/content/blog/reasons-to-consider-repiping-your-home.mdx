Repiping — or water line replacement — is the process of replacing the water supply lines that run through your home. It's not a small project, but for the right home it can prevent years of leaks, pressure problems, and water damage. Here's how to know if it's worth considering.

## The Age of Your Home

Homes built in the 1970s were commonly plumbed with galvanized steel pipes, and those pipes are now well past the point where failure becomes likely. Galvanized steel is prone to internal erosion over time, and eventually it bursts.

## Reduced Water Pressure

As galvanized pipes age, mineral deposits and corrosion build up on the inside, narrowing the passage and restricting flow. A related symptom worth watching for: if turning on one fixture causes the water temperature to swing somewhere else in the house, your supply lines may be struggling to keep up.

## Polybutylene Piping

If your home was built between 1978 and 1995, especially in the Pacific Northwest, there's a real chance it was plumbed with polybutylene — a plastic pipe material that was popular at the time but has proven prone to sudden failure. Over the years its structural integrity breaks down, becoming weak and unstable until it fails and causes water damage inside the home.

## Corroded Copper Plumbing

Even copper isn't permanent. Its lifespan depends on the type: Type K copper can last 50 years or more, Type L typically holds up for 25 to 30 years, and Type M tends to last around 20 years before corrosion becomes a real concern.

## Not Sure What You Have?

If you don't know what your home is plumbed with — or you're already seeing signs of trouble — a licensed plumber can assess your system and let you know whether full [repiping](/repiping/) makes sense or whether a more targeted repair will do.
