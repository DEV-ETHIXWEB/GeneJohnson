Hard water — water with a high mineral content, mainly calcium and magnesium — affects an estimated three-fourths of homes in the United States. Beyond the chalky stains it leaves behind, it can also take a toll on your plumbing and fixtures over time.

## Cleaning Glass and Tile

Soap scum, mildew, and hard water spots on glass and tile can usually be cleaned with an acid-based cleaner and a nylon-backed sponge.

## Cleaning Food-Contact Surfaces

For surfaces that come into contact with food, a vinegar or salt water solution is a safer alternative to harsher acidic cleaners.

## Cleaning Showers

Spray a vinegar solution on shower walls and doors, then simply run the shower rather than manually scrubbing — the water helps rinse away the loosened mineral deposits.

## Cleaning Porcelain Fixtures

A powdered all-purpose cleaner paired with a fine-grit drywall sanding screen can lift hard water stains from porcelain without scratching the surface.

## Cleaning Marble

Marble calls for a gentler touch — mild soap applied with a soft towel or fine steel wool is usually enough.

## Cleaning Your Car

For water spots on a vehicle, mix equal parts vinegar and distilled water for an effective, gentle solution.

## The Long-Term Fix

All of these methods work, but they only treat the symptom — the stains will keep coming back as long as your water stays hard.
