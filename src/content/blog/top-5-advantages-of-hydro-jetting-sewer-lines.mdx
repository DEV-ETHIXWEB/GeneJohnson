When a sewer line clog resists everything else, [hydro jetting](/hydrojetting/) is usually the next step. The process uses high-pressure water streams to blast through blockages and clean the interior of the pipe — and plumbers typically confirm it's the right approach with a video camera inspection before starting. Here's why it's worth considering.

## 1. Thorough Cleaning

The sheer force of the water pressure clears accumulated obstructions in a single treatment, making it especially effective on stubborn blockages that shrug off other methods like snaking.

## 2. Environmentally Friendly

Unlike chemical drain cleaners, hydro jetting uses nothing but water. It also tends to last longer, because it fully breaks up the clog rather than just punching a temporary hole through the middle of it.

## 3. Prevents Future Problems

By clearing out dirt, debris, and scale buildup along the pipe walls — not just the clog itself — hydro jetting reduces the risk of bigger problems down the line, including burst pipes or a full [sewer line replacement](/sewer-line-repair-replacement/).

## 4. Reaches Difficult Areas

The method works regardless of your home's layout or the age of the pipes, and it doesn't require extensive digging to access the line. That keeps both the cost and the disruption to your property much lower than more invasive repairs.

## 5. Serious Power

Hydro jetting is the most powerful tool plumbers have for cleaning pipes — powerful enough to clear tree roots and small rocks without damaging the pipe itself.

## Is It Right for Your Home?

Hydro jetting isn't the answer for every clog, which is why a camera inspection matters before committing to it.
