When flu season hits your household, keeping one sick family member from infecting everyone else takes more than good intentions — it takes a few deliberate habits. The flu spreads through respiratory droplets that can travel four to six feet through a cough or sneeze, and it lingers on surfaces like doorknobs and keyboards long after that.

## Isolate the Sick Person

Limiting contact between the sick family member and everyone else for about seven days after symptoms begin gives the virus far fewer opportunities to jump to a new host. It's not always practical to fully quarantine someone at home, but even reducing shared space and close contact makes a real difference.

## Cover Coughs and Sneezes

The sick person should use a tissue or the crook of the elbow when coughing or sneezing, and dispose of used tissues immediately rather than leaving them around. This one habit alone dramatically cuts down on airborne droplet spread.

## Practice Consistent Hygiene

Frequent handwashing matters for everyone in the house — the sick person should avoid touching shared food or surfaces, and other family members should wash their hands after handling anything the sick person has touched. Avoid touching your face in between, since that's one of the easiest ways germs travel from a surface to your system.

## Disinfect High-Touch Surfaces

Clean and disinfect the surfaces most likely to carry the virus: sinks, toilets, kitchen items, counters, desks, and door knobs. These are the spots germs settle on and wait for the next hand that touches them.

## Watch Out for Vulnerable Household Members

Take extra precautions for anyone 65 or older, or with a chronic condition like diabetes, heart disease, or lung disease — the flu tends to hit these groups harder.

## Don't Overlook Your HVAC System

Your heating and cooling system plays a bigger role in this than most people realize. Talk to an HVAC professional about improving your home's [air quality](/air-quality/) during flu season — better filtration and humidity control can support the rest of these habits rather than work against them.
