It's tempting to grab a bottle of drain cleaner off the store shelf the moment a sink starts backing up. Chemical drain cleaners may offer a quick fix, but they often create bigger problems down the road. Professional [hydro jetting](/hydrojetting/) is the safer, more effective alternative — here's why.

## What's Actually in Chemical Drain Cleaners

These products rely on harsh substances like lye, sulfuric acid, or bleach to dissolve blockages, generating heat and a chemical reaction inside the pipe. The problem is that they can't tell the difference between the clog and the pipe itself. Over repeated use, they corrode pipes, damage seals, shorten your plumbing's lifespan, and pose safety risks to people, pets, and the environment.

## How Hydro Jetting Works Instead

Hydro jetting uses highly pressurized water — often exceeding 3,000 psi — to blast away blockages and buildup. Where a chemical cleaner just burns a small hole through a clog, hydro jetting completely scours the pipe walls, restoring them close to their original condition. A specialized nozzle removes grease, roots, soap scum, and scale deposits, all without a single corrosive chemical.

## The Risks of Going Chemical

Chemical cleaners can erode PVC or metal pipes over time, leading to leaks or bursts. The fumes irritate lungs and eyes, and splashes can cause chemical burns. Worse, they typically only offer temporary relief — since they burn through the center of a clog rather than removing it, the problem often returns quickly. They also contaminate groundwater, harm aquatic life, and can disrupt the bacterial balance in a septic system.

## Why Hydro Jetting Wins Long-Term

Hydro jetting removes buildup completely rather than just punching through it, which means fewer repeat clogs. It's water-only, so there's no environmental contamination, and when pressure is professionally calibrated, it's safe for your pipes. It's also one of the few methods effective against tree roots, mineral scale, and genuinely tough blockages that a bottle of cleaner will never touch.

## What It Costs You Either Way

A bottle of chemical cleaner runs about $10, but repeated use can lead to pipe damage that costs thousands to repair. Hydro jetting costs more upfront but delivers a long-term solution, meaning fewer service calls and a longer life for your plumbing system.
