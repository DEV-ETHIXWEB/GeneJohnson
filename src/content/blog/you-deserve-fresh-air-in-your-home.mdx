Most of us spend the majority of our time indoors, which means the air circulating through your home has a bigger impact on your health than you might think. Dust, pet dander, mold spores, and dust mites are constantly accumulating in carpets, furniture, and forgotten corners of the house - and for anyone with allergies or asthma, that buildup can make daily life miserable.

## Know What You're Breathing

Allergens are tiny protein particles tied to bacteria, pet dander, mold, and dust mites. Household dust itself is a mix of skin flakes, pet hair, fabric fibers, and carpet particles, all of which give dust mites plenty to feed on. The result is an indoor environment that can quietly aggravate allergy and asthma symptoms year-round, especially during Seattle's damp winters when windows stay shut.

## Rethink Your Furniture and Flooring

Carpeting and heavily upholstered furniture trap far more allergens than hard surfaces do. Where it makes sense, leather furniture and wood or tile flooring make it much easier to keep contaminant levels down, since there's less material for dust and dander to cling to.

## Check the Spaces You Forget

Unfinished basements and attics are prime spots for moisture, mold, and pest activity to take hold unnoticed. A periodic inspection of these areas can catch a developing air quality problem before it spreads into the rest of the house.

## Build a Cleaning Routine That Works

- Dust and vacuum regularly, ideally with a HEPA-filter vacuum that actually captures fine particles instead of recirculating them
- Use sanitizing cleaners on high-touch, high-dust surfaces
- Ask household members to remove shoes at the door to keep outdoor contaminants from tracking in
- Stick to a consistent schedule rather than cleaning only when it's visibly needed

## When Cleaning Isn't Enough

Sometimes good habits alone can't keep up with a home's air quality needs, particularly in older houses with limited ventilation. A whole-home [air quality](/air-quality/) solution - filtration, purification, or improved ventilation - can handle what a vacuum and a dust rag can't.
