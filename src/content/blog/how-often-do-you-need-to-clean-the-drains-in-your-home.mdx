Drains rarely get attention until they stop working, but by then you're usually dealing with standing water, odors, or worse. A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding sewage backups, pipe corrosion, and the structural damage that comes with letting buildup go unchecked.

## What affects how often you need to clean

There's no single answer for every home. Frequency depends on:

- How many people live in the house and how much water they use
- The type of drain - kitchen drains work harder than most
- The age and condition of your plumbing
- Personal habits, like how much hair, grease, or product residue ends up going down the drain

## A realistic cleaning schedule

- **Kitchen sinks:** monthly at minimum, every two to three weeks if you cook often
- **Bathroom sinks and tubs:** quarterly
- **Shower drains:** annually
- **Toilet drains:** every six to twelve months
- **Floor drains:** annually
- **Washing machine drains:** a bio-cleaning treatment every few months

## DIY options worth trying

Baking soda and vinegar flushes, mechanical drain snakes, and enzyme-based cleaners are all reasonable, affordable ways to keep buildup from becoming a full clog. They're not a substitute for professional cleaning on a badly neglected drain, but they're a good habit between visits.

## When to bring in a professional

If you've just moved into an older home, if a drain keeps clogging despite regular DIY maintenance, or if you simply want a clear picture of what's going on in your pipes, a licensed plumber can run a video inspection and perform [hydrojetting](/hydrojetting/) to clear out buildup that a snake can't reach. It's a good habit to have your [drains](/drains/) professionally assessed periodically, especially if your home is older or you've never had it done before - it's a lot cheaper than dealing with a backup after the fact.
