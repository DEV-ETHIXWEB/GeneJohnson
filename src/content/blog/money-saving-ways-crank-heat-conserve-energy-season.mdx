Staying warm all winter doesn't have to mean watching your heating bill climb every month. A few low-cost adjustments around the house can meaningfully cut how hard your furnace has to work.

## Reverse your ceiling fans

Most people only think about ceiling fans in summer, but flipping them to run clockwise in cold months makes a real difference. The movement of the fan, combined with the rising warm air, creates an energy-efficient downdraft that pushes heat back down into the room instead of letting it pool near the ceiling. The one exception is cathedral or vaulted ceilings, which should keep running clockwise... er, counterclockwise year-round - check your fan's manual if you're unsure which direction is which for your model.

## Seal windows and doors

Small cracks around window frames are an easy way for heat to escape unnoticed. Caulking gaps and adding weather-stripping to doorways so the door sits flush with the entryway creates a much more airtight seal, keeping warm air in without any change to your furnace.

## Insulate your windows

Two simple options here: applying window insulation film, pressed into place with the heat from a hairdryer, or installing solar shield blinds. Both help convert the sun's heat into radiant heat during the day, reducing how much you need to turn up the furnace to stay comfortable.

## Small investment, real savings

None of these fixes require professional installation or a big budget, but together they can meaningfully soften the shock of a winter heating bill.
