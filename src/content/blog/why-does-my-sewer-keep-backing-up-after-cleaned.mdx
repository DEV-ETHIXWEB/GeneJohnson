If you've had your sewer line cleaned recently and the backups are already returning, it's a frustrating and confusing situation. Cleaning is supposed to fix the problem — so why is it back? The honest answer is that cleaning removes a blockage, but it doesn't fix whatever caused that blockage to form in the first place.

## Underlying Pipe Damage

Older clay or cast-iron pipes develop rough interiors, flaking, and small fractures over time. Those imperfections act like a net, snagging toilet paper and debris that would otherwise flow through smoothly. A cleaning clears what's there, but the pipe surface immediately starts collecting material again.

## Tree Root Intrusion

In our experience across the Seattle area, root intrusion is one of the most common reasons a sewer line clogs again and again. A cleaning cuts the roots back temporarily, but it doesn't stop them from growing back into the same cracks. If your backups tend to follow heavy rain, or you're dealing with slow drains throughout the house and recurring clogs every few months, roots are a likely suspect. Learn more about [how tree roots invade drains](/drains/) and what can be done about it.

## Bellied Pipes and Misaligned Joints

A "bellied" section of pipe is a low spot where waste and water collect instead of draining away, and misaligned joints create similar collection points. No amount of cleaning fixes a structural sag in the line — the pipe itself needs to be repaired or replaced.

## Debris That Doesn't Belong

Products labeled "flushable," along with grease, paper towels, and feminine hygiene products, don't break down the way toilet paper does. In an older or already-compromised pipe, they can rebuild a clog within weeks of a cleaning.

## Not All Cleaning Is Equal

A basic snaking punches a hole through a clog, while [hydrojetting](/hydrojetting/) uses high-pressure water to scour the full interior of the pipe. If your line keeps clogging quickly after a snake, a jetting service may extend the time between problems significantly.

## Get an Actual Diagnosis

The only way to know for sure what's happening inside your line is a camera inspection. It shows exactly where the pipe is damaged, bellied, or root-infested, so you're not guessing at a solution.
