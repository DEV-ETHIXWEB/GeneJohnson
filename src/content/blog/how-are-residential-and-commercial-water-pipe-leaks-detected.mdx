Plumbing systems themselves haven't changed dramatically over the decades, but the way leaks and failures get diagnosed certainly has. Where plumbers once had to cut open drywall or excavate a yard just to find a leak, modern detection technology can now pinpoint the problem with far less disruption.

## Endoscopic Detection

This method uses a small, flexible camera that can be threaded into tight spaces to visually identify cracks, breaks, or other imperfections inside a pipe. It's especially useful in confined areas, and it can also help rule out drain clogs that sometimes mimic the symptoms of a real leak.

## Ultrasonic Detection

Ultrasonic equipment listens for the sound water makes as it escapes a pipe and translates that noise into a digital map a plumber can follow to the source. It's particularly valuable for leaks under a slab or foundation where a camera simply can't reach. Higher water pressure produces a louder, clearer signal, and the sensitivity can be adjusted as the technician gets closer to the leak.

## Thermographic Detection

Thermal imaging cameras measure infrared energy to detect subtle temperature differences between leaking water and the surrounding material. This makes it especially effective for leaks hidden behind walls or under floors — the kind that typically show up first as discoloration or a damp patch that won't go away.

## Why This Matters for You

These non-invasive methods mean a suspected leak can usually be confirmed and located without demolition, saving you time, money, and mess.
