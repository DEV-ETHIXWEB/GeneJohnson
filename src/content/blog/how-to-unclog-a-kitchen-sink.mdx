A backed-up kitchen sink is one of the most common plumbing headaches in Seattle homes, and often one of the easiest to fix yourself - if you know where to start.

## What's Actually Causing the Clog

Kitchen sink clogs usually come from one of three sources: food particles, non-food items that shouldn't have gone down the drain in the first place (paper towels, small toys), or grease buildup. Grease is a sneaky one - liquid fats might initially make it down the drain, but they can solidify once they cool off, narrowing the pipe from the inside out.

## DIY Methods Worth Trying

- **Plunger** - create a tight seal over the drain and use steady, even pressure
- **Baking soda and vinegar** - pour about half a cup of baking soda down the drain followed by vinegar, and let the reaction work at the clog
- **Boiling water** - run hot water for 5-10 minutes, optionally following it with a bit of dish soap
- **Manual removal** - a plumber's snake or a straightened wire coat hanger can pull out visible debris

## When to Call a Professional

If none of these work, especially in an older Seattle home with aging pipes, it's time to bring in a professional. We use tools like [hydrojetting](/hydrojetting/) and cable machines to clear stubborn clogs without damaging the pipe.

## Preventing the Next Clog

- Never scrape food scraps into the drain
- Keep grease and oils out of the pipes entirely
- Run hot water after each use
- Do a monthly baking soda and vinegar flush as routine maintenance
