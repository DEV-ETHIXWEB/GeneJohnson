Switching to low-flow fixtures is one of the simplest ways to reduce your household's water use - and your water bill - without changing how your home actually feels to live in.

## Low-Flow Showerheads

A standard showerhead can use roughly 60 liters of water in just eight minutes. Low-flow showerheads cut that volume in one of two ways: aerating models mix air into the water stream to preserve the feeling of pressure, while non-aerating models force water through smaller openings to create a higher-pressure spray using less total water. Either approach can meaningfully cut your shower's water use.

## Low-Flow Toilets

Older toilets can use several times more water per flush than a modern low-flow model, which typically uses around 6 liters. There are two main types: gravity-flush toilets, common in most homes, which rely on the weight of tank water to clear the bowl, and pressure-assist toilets, often used commercially or in homes with weaker drainage, which use compressed air to clear waste forcefully with less water.

## Low-Flow Faucets

Low-flow faucet aerators reduce water flow while maintaining the feel of normal water pressure. They're available in a few flow rates, commonly 1.0 and 2.2 gallons per minute - the lower rate delivers the bigger savings.
