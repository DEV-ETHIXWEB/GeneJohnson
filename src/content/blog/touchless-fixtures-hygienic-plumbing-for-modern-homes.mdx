Touchless fixtures used to be something you only saw in commercial restrooms, but they've become increasingly common in Seattle-area homes. These are plumbing features that operate through motion sensors instead of physical handles or buttons, and beyond the convenience factor, they offer real hygiene and efficiency benefits. Here's a look at the options worth considering.

## Touchless Faucets

A motion sensor activates the flow of water the moment your hands appear beneath it, and shuts it off the moment you step away. Beyond cutting down on shared-surface germs, this also reduces water waste from faucets left running.

## Touchless Toilets

Motion sensors near the tank trigger the flush automatically, and many models offer dual-flush settings to conserve water on lighter uses. It's a small change that removes one more shared touchpoint from the bathroom.

## Touchless Soap Dispensers

An infrared sensor dispenses soap without needing to press a pump, which keeps dirty hands from contaminating the countertop or the dispenser itself.

## Touchless Flush Plates

For homes that want a sleeker look without replacing the whole toilet, wall-mounted sensor plates can replace a traditional flush handle while giving the bathroom a more modern feel.

## Touchless Water Bottle Fillers

These station-style fixtures, often paired with a built-in filter, make it easy to refill a water bottle without touching a handle - a nice option for kitchens or mudrooms in busy households.

## Touchless Shower Controls

Smart shower systems let you pre-set a water temperature and start the shower remotely through an app, so you can step into water that's already at the right temperature.

## What to Consider Before Installing

Touchless fixtures typically need a power source, whether that's a battery or a hardwired connection, so placement and access matter. It's also worth thinking through sensor placement, ongoing maintenance, and your overall budget before committing to a full bathroom or kitchen upgrade.
