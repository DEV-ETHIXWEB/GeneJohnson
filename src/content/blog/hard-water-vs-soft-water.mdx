If you've ever noticed a cloudy film on your shower doors or dishes that never quite come out spotless, hard water is probably the reason. Here's what separates hard water from soft water, and what to do if hard water is affecting your home.

## What Makes Water "Hard"

Hard water gets its name from its mineral content — mainly calcium and magnesium picked up as water travels through soil and rock before reaching your home. Soft water, by contrast, has had those minerals removed or was never carrying much of them to begin with.

## The Everyday Problems Hard Water Causes

**Cloudy residue** is one of the most visible signs. As water dries on shower doors, walls, and dishware, it leaves behind mineral deposits that build into a stubborn film over time.

**Dull hair and faded fabric** are less obvious but just as common. Hard water can leave hair looking flat and lifeless, while accelerating the fading and wear of clothing washed in it.

**Poor lathering** is another telltale sign — the minerals in hard water interfere with soap's ability to form a proper lather, often leaving a filmy residue instead of clean suds.

## The Fix: A Water Softener

A water softener addresses the problem at the source through ion exchange: sodium ions are exchanged with the calcium and magnesium ions in your water supply, effectively removing what makes the water "hard" in the first place. The result is water that lathers properly, leaves less residue, and is gentler on both your home and your skin.
