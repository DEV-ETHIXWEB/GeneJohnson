Summer in the Seattle area can sneak up fast, and the worst time to discover your air conditioner isn't working is the first genuinely hot afternoon of the year. A little preparation in spring saves you from waiting in a service queue with everyone else who had the same idea.

## Start With What You Learned Last Winter

If your heating system gave you trouble over the colder months, don't let those notes disappear. Jotting down issues while they're fresh makes it much easier to schedule the right repairs before fall — and often those same ducts and airflow paths affect your cooling performance too.

## Install and Secure Your Units

If you use window or portable units, get them installed early and make sure they're mounted securely. A poorly secured unit isn't just inefficient — it's a safety hazard and, in the case of window units, a potential break-in point if it isn't properly locked into place.

## Clean or Replace Filters

Dirty filters are one of the simplest things to fix and one of the most commonly ignored. A clogged filter reduces airflow, forces your system to work harder, and can even become a fire hazard in extreme cases. Start the season with a clean filter and check it monthly through the summer.

## Check Your Ductwork

Before you rely on your system for the season, inspect accessible ductwork for holes or gaps. Leaky ducts waste conditioned air and drive up your cooling costs without you ever noticing — the air just never quite reaches the room.

## Test Early, Not During a Heat Wave

Run your [cooling](/cooling/ac-maintenance/) system on a mild day in March or April rather than waiting for the first 90-degree afternoon. Testing early gives you time to catch problems — low refrigerant, a struggling thermostat, uneven cooling — and get them fixed before demand (and wait times) spike.

## Keep Up With Maintenance

Annual maintenance is the difference between a small adjustment now and an emergency repair later. Regular check-ups let a technician catch small issues before they become expensive ones, and they keep your system running efficiently all season long.
