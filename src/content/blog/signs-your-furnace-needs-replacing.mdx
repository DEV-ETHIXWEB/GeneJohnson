Every furnace eventually reaches a point where continuing to repair it costs more, in total, than replacing it. The trick is recognizing that point before you've sunk money into a system on its way out.

## The age threshold

Most gas furnaces are designed to last 15-20 years. Once a furnace passes 15 years, we generally recommend weighing any repair over roughly a third of replacement cost against simply replacing the unit - the remaining components are aging in parallel, and a fixed part today often just delays the next failure.

## The "rule of $5,000"

A common rule of thumb: multiply the age of the furnace by the cost of the repair. If that number exceeds $5,000, replacement is usually the better financial call. A $600 repair on a 10-year-old furnace (6,000) leans toward replacement; the same repair on a 5-year-old unit (3,000) leans toward fixing it.

## Warning signs beyond age

- **A cracked heat exchanger** - this is a safety issue, not just a performance one, and typically means replacement rather than repair.
- **Rising energy bills with no usage change** - declining efficiency often shows up on the utility bill before it shows up as a breakdown.
- **Repeated repairs in a single season** - if you've called for service more than twice this winter, the system is telling you something.
- **Uneven heating between rooms** - this can point to a failing blower or a furnace that's undersized or overworked for the home.

## What to do next

If your furnace is showing two or more of these signs, it's worth getting a real load calculation and replacement quote alongside the repair estimate - not to upsell you, but so you're comparing the actual total cost of each path.
