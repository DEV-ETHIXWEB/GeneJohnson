Seattle gets enough rain in a given year that outdoor drainage isn't optional — it's the difference between a yard that sheds water properly and one that turns into standing puddles, soggy soil, or worse, water finding its way into your foundation. Here's a rundown of the outdoor drain types you're likely to run into, and what each one is actually good for.

## French Drains

A gravel-filled trench with a perforated pipe running through it, designed to capture excess groundwater and surface runoff before it pools. French drains are affordable and blend easily into landscaping, though the gravel does accumulate sediment over time and needs occasional attention.

## Trench Drains

Long, narrow channels topped with a metal grate, built to handle heavy water flow off driveways and patios during intense storms. They work fast, but installation involves real excavation, and the grates need regular clearing to keep debris from backing things up.

## Catch Basins

Square grated inlets that collect surface runoff and route it into the storm drain system. They remove standing water quickly, but they need to be installed with the right slope, or they'll clog instead of draining.

## Channel Drains

A wider, linear version of a trench drain, used across large paved areas that still need to handle foot traffic. They manage high flow rates well, but precise grading during installation is essential — and that adds to the labor cost.

## Downspout Drains

These carry roof runoff away from your gutters and route it well clear of the foundation, which helps prevent both soil oversaturation and basement leaks. If your downspouts currently just dump water a foot from the house, this is often the cheapest drainage upgrade with the biggest payoff.

## Dry Wells

Underground gravel-filled chambers that let rainwater slowly percolate back into the surrounding soil. They're affordable and help recharge groundwater naturally, though they can overflow in a severe storm or underperform in soil that doesn't drain well to begin with.

## Choosing the Right One

Most properties end up using a combination of these systems rather than just one.
