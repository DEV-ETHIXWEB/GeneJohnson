A [tankless water heater](/tankless-water-heaters/) is built to run for years with minimal fuss, but it isn't maintenance-free. Because it's working harder and more often than a traditional tank unit, small problems tend to show up as noticeable symptoms before they become expensive failures. Here's what to watch for.

## Inconsistent Hot Water

If your water arrives cold, lukewarm, or with noticeably weaker output than usual, mineral buildup, a failing heat exchanger, or a blocked vent are the likely culprits - all of which need a professional look.

## Temperature Swings Mid-Shower

Water temperature that jumps between hot and cold usually points to a flow sensor or temperature controller issue, often triggered by sediment buildup. Beyond the annoyance, this can actually be a safety concern for kids or elderly family members in the shower.

## Weaker Water Pressure

Mineral scale narrows the internal pathways of the unit - trying to push water through them starts to feel like pushing water through a straw filled with sand. Pump problems or clogged filters can cause the same symptom.

## Banging, Whistling, or Rumbling Noises

Unusual sounds from the unit are a sign of scale accumulation or a component under stress. They're easy to ignore because they're intermittent, but they tend to get worse, not better, on their own.

## Error Codes or Warning Lights

Modern tankless units are designed to flag developing problems before they become full failures. Don't dismiss an error code - check the manual or call a professional to diagnose it properly.

## Discolored or Foul-Smelling Water

This can indicate corrosion inside the unit or bacterial growth, both of which are worth addressing quickly for the sake of the equipment and your health.

## A Rising Energy Bill

If your gas or electric bill is climbing without an obvious explanation, your tankless unit may be working harder than it should to deliver the same amount of hot water - a classic sign of declining efficiency.

## The Fix Is Usually Simple

Most of these issues trace back to mineral scale, which is exactly why annual maintenance matters so much for tankless systems. A yearly flush and inspection catches these problems while they're still a quick fix rather than a costly repair.
