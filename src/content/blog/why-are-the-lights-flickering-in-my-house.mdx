Flickering lights are one of those problems that's easy to shrug off - until it happens every night. The causes range from harmless to genuinely dangerous, so it's worth understanding what's actually going on.

## Electrical issues

Loose wiring connections at outlets and junction boxes, a faulty breaker or fuse box, an overloaded circuit, or simply outdated wiring can all cause the lights to flicker inconsistently throughout the house.

## Appliance problems

High-demand devices like air conditioners and refrigerators can cause voltage dips when they cycle on, which shows up as a brief flicker elsewhere in the house. Faulty electronics with internal shorts, or motors struggling during startup, can cause the same effect.

## External factors

Sometimes it's not your house at all. Power grid inconsistencies from the utility company, weather events affecting exterior lines, or utility maintenance work nearby can all cause temporary flickering that has nothing to do with your home's wiring.

## Lighting fixture issues

Loose bulbs, incompatible bulb types or wattages, and faulty switches or dimmers are some of the simplest - and easiest to fix - causes.

## What you can check yourself

Carefully remove bulbs and make sure they're properly screwed in with no wiggle room. From there, check other connections, try swapping bulbs, reset the relevant circuit breaker, and unplug any large appliances that might be pulling power at the same time as the flickering.

## When to stop and call a professional

Flickering tied to wiring or the panel isn't a DIY project - the risks include electrical fire, shock, and appliance damage, on top of the daily annoyance. Don't mess around with wiring or panel issues without professional expertise.
