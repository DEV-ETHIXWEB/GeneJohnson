Plumbing systems are generally reliable, but things can go wrong from time to time - a burst line, a clogged toilet that won't stop overflowing, a sink that just keeps running. When it happens, staying calm and knowing the right first steps makes a real difference in how much damage your home takes on.

## Safety comes first

If a water leak is anywhere near an electrical outlet or appliance, that's the priority. If a water leak impacts an electrical or gas-powered device, it is important to get everyone out of the home and to turn off the electricity and/or gas before doing anything else. No repair is worth risking a shock or a gas hazard.

## Shut off the water

Once safety is handled, the next step is cutting off the water supply. Most fixtures - toilets, sinks, faucets - have a local shutoff valve nearby; turning that off will often stop the immediate flooding. If you can't find one or the leak is coming from somewhere without an isolated valve, the water supply should be turned off to the whole home at the main shutoff or meter.

## Call for help right away

Every minute a leak runs unchecked adds to the water damage and the eventual repair cost. The quicker you call, the quicker we can get there and get the problem fixed. Don't wait until morning or "see if it stops" - a fast call is almost always the cheaper outcome.
