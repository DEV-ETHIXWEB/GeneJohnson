When your HVAC system suddenly stops working, it's rarely a mystery with no explanation — it's almost always one of a handful of familiar culprits. Here's what tends to cause breakdowns in Seattle homes, and what to watch for.

## Skipped maintenance

Not keeping your HVAC unit maintained is one of the most common causes of breakdowns we see. Skipping filter changes or an annual service visit lets small problems compound quietly until the system fails outright — often at the worst possible time.

## Refrigerant leaks

Refrigerant is the chemical that actually does the cooling, and a leak means the unit can no longer bring air down to the temperature you've set. If your system is running constantly but the air coming out isn't cold enough, a leak is a likely cause and needs a licensed technician to locate and repair.

## A malfunctioning thermostat

A faulty thermostat can prevent your system from turning on correctly, or keep it from running for the right amount of time. If your [thermostat](/thermostats/) settings and your home's actual temperature never seem to match, that mismatch is often the first clue.

## Sensor failures

If a sensor isn't working properly, your unit may not know when it should come on or shut off, resulting in inconsistent heating or cooling throughout the home.

## A blocked condenser coil

Dirt and debris on the outdoor condenser coil reduce how well the system transfers heat. A gentle rinse with a garden hose can clear minor buildup, but persistent problems call for a professional inspection.

## When to call in a professional

Most of these issues start small and are far cheaper to fix early than after a full breakdown. Scheduling an annual inspection with a certified technician catches emerging problems before they leave you without heat or [cooling](/cooling/ac-maintenance/).
