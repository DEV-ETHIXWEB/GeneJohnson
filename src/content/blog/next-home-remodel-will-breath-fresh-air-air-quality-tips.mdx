A home remodel is exciting, but it can quietly wreck your indoor air quality if you're not careful. Smoking, fireplaces, and pet dander already put a strain on the air inside your home - and tearing into walls, floors, or cabinetry adds a whole new layer of risk. Renovations can expose hidden contaminants like mold, vermin droppings, and old pet urine, while sanding, painting, and varnishing release dust and fumes that linger long after the crew goes home.

## Plan Before You Swing the First Hammer

Before demolition starts, think about which parts of your home need to stay livable. If the project is in your kitchen or a bedroom, consider eating out more often, sleeping in another room, or staying with family for a few nights. Keeping distance between your daily life and the work zone is the simplest way to avoid breathing in what the renovation kicks up.

## Contain the Mess

Sealing off the work area does more good than most homeowners expect. Hang floor-to-ceiling plastic sheeting around the renovation zone to trap dust and debris, and tape off doorways so particles can't drift into the rest of the house. In the room being worked on, open windows to vent fumes outside rather than let them recirculate. It's also smart to shut off your HVAC system while active work is underway - otherwise your ductwork can pull in dust and odors and spread them to every room in the house.

## Don't Forget the Filter

Once the dust settles, your air filter is doing more work than usual. A well-maintained HVAC system can cut energy costs by roughly 15% a year, but that only holds true if the filter is clean. Change it at least twice a year under normal conditions, and more often - every couple of months - if the renovation kicked up extra dust or outdoor air quality has been poor.
