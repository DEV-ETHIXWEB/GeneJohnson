Cutting your summer cooling bill doesn't always mean buying new equipment. A few of the most effective changes are things you can do today, for free, and a couple of them might surprise you.

## Close the Blinds

Sunlight streaming through windows converts to heat once it's inside, and that heat is exactly what your AC is working to remove. Closing blinds and windows during the hottest parts of the day keeps that heat from ever getting in, so the unit doesn't have to cycle on as often. If you're repainting anytime soon, lighter wall colors help too, since they absorb less heat than darker ones.

## Adjust Your AC's Fan Speed

Running your system's fan at a lower speed pulls more humidity out of the air as it passes over the coils. That matters more than people expect - humidity makes air feel hotter than the thermometer says, and a good system running at the right speed can make 77 degrees feel closer to 73.

## Skip the Fans When the AC Is Running

This one trips people up: turning on a fan doesn't actually lower the temperature. Worse, exhaust fans in bathrooms and kitchens actively pull climate-controlled air out of the house while the AC is running, which just makes your system work harder to replace it. Ceiling fans are generally fine as long as your windows stay closed.

## Upgrade to Double-Pane Windows

Heat always moves toward cooler areas, and most of the heat entering your home during summer comes in through the windows. Double-pane windows provide meaningfully better insulation than single-pane, and while the upfront cost is real, the long-term savings add up.

## Invest in a Good Thermostat

A quality [thermostat](/thermostats/) regulates exactly when your system cycles on and off, avoiding the wasted runs that come from an imprecise or outdated model. Over a full season, that precision translates into a lower bill.
