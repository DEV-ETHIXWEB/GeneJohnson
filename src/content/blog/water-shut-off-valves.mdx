Here's a simple piece of advice that saves homeowners real money and real stress: everyone should know how to shut off the water to individual sinks, toilets, washing machines, and other water-using appliances like the icemaker.

## Why It Matters More Than You'd Think

When a supply line fails or a fixture starts leaking, every second counts. Knowing exactly where the shut-off valve is for that specific sink, toilet, or appliance means you can stop the flow immediately, instead of scrambling to find the main shut-off while water keeps spreading across your floor.

## Faster Response, Less Damage

Understanding where and how to shut off water at the fixture level can reduce the potential damage and take some of the sting out of your cleanup efforts. It's a targeted approach — you don't have to cut water to the entire house just to deal with a leaking dishwasher line or a running toilet.

## Take Five Minutes This Week

Walk through your home and locate the shut-off valves under every sink, behind the toilet, and near the washing machine and refrigerator. Test that each one actually turns — valves that haven't been touched in years sometimes seize up, which is worth knowing before an emergency, not during one.
