A toilet tank that won't refill after a flush is more than an inconvenience — it usually points to a specific mechanical failure inside the tank. Here are the most common causes we find.

## Float and fill valve problems

The float is a hollow ballast that monitors water level; cracks in it or an obstructed lever can prevent the tank from refilling automatically after a flush. Closely related is the fill valve itself, which controls incoming water flow. Worn components, corrosion, or mineral buildup can block water from passing through — fill valves typically last around 3–5 years before these issues start showing up.

## Water supply issues

Low municipal water pressure, mineral buildup inside the pipes, a kinked supply line, or a clog upstream can all restrict how much water actually reaches the tank.

## Trip assembly and tank damage

A damaged trip assembly — the handle-and-lever mechanism that triggers the flush — can fail due to rust, bent components, or a broken chain, preventing the tank from draining and refilling properly. Separately, thermal stress from repeated heating and cooling cycles can weaken porcelain over time, causing hairline cracks that slowly drain the tank's water.

## Overflow tube and flapper issues

A blocked overflow tube eliminates the tank's built-in safety overflow pathway. A worn flapper valve — especially one with mineral deposits caked onto its underside — won't seal properly, letting water seep out prematurely.

## Vent blockages

Clogged toilet vents can create vacuum pressure that actually siphons water out of the tank through the bowl, a less obvious cause that often gets missed in DIY troubleshooting.

## DIY versus calling a plumber

Simple fixes like adjusting a float or swapping a flapper are reasonable DIY jobs. But for severe water damage, ventilation-related issues, or anything unfamiliar, it's worth calling in a professional.
