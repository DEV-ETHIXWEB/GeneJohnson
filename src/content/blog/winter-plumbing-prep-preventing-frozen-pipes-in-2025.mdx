Seattle doesn't get the brutal winters some parts of the country do, but that's exactly why frozen pipes catch so many homeowners here off guard. A little prep before the cold arrives goes a long way toward avoiding a burst pipe in the middle of a cold snap.

## Insulation is your first line of defense

Pipe insulation is the single most effective thing you can do, particularly for pipes running through basements, crawlspaces, attics, and garages - the parts of the house that don't get much ambient heat. Foam pipe sleeves, fiberglass wrap, and heat tape are all solid, affordable options. While you're at it, seal up drafts around the foundation, windows, and utility openings with caulk, spray foam, or weatherstripping - cold air finding its way in is often what puts pipes at risk in the first place.

## Keep the temperature steady

Consistent indoor temperatures, day and night, protect vulnerable pipes far better than cranking the heat only when someone's home. If you're traveling, keep the thermostat at 55°F or higher rather than shutting the heat off entirely - it's a small cost compared to a burst pipe.

## A few simple habits

- Let faucets drip slightly during extreme cold snaps to relieve pressure buildup in the line.
- Disconnect garden hoses and shut off outdoor faucets before the first freeze.
- Add extra insulation to crawlspace vents and exposed attic pipes.

## Get ahead of it with an inspection

Scheduling a plumbing inspection before winter arrives lets us catch vulnerabilities - a poorly insulated run, an aging fitting - while they're still minor and easy to fix.

## Signs a pipe may already be freezing

Watch for reduced water flow, frost visible on exposed pipes, unusual odors from drains, or strange noises when you run water. If you notice any of these, don't attempt to thaw a pipe yourself - that's how bursts happen. Call a licensed plumber right away, or reach out for [emergency plumbing service](/emergency-plumbing/) if you're already seeing signs of a leak.
