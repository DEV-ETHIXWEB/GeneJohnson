Seattle's rainy season puts real strain on a home's plumbing and drainage systems, and a little preparation in the fall goes a long way toward avoiding a flooded basement or a backed-up sewer line in the middle of winter. Here's what we recommend checking before the heavy rains arrive.

## Clean Out the Gutters

Debris-clogged gutters send water pouring down your siding and pooling right next to your foundation instead of away from it. Clear them out at least twice a year, and make sure downspouts extend several feet away from the house.

## Check Your Yard's Drainage

Walk your property after a rain and look for soggy patches or standing puddles that don't drain within a few hours. Poor yard drainage can often be corrected by regrading the soil or installing a French drain in the problem area.

## Test Basement Drains

Pour water into basement floor drains to confirm it flows freely, and clear any debris sitting in the grates. A slow basement drain now can mean a flooded basement later.

## Inspect Your Sump Pump

Pour water directly into the [sump pump](/sump-pumps/) pit to confirm the pump activates and discharges properly. If you don't already have a battery backup system, rainy season is the time to add one — power outages and heavy storms tend to happen together.

## Have Your Sewer Line Checked

Gurgling drains, slow drainage in multiple fixtures, or sewage odors are all signs your [sewer line](/sewer-line-repair-replacement/) may need attention before it's put to the test by heavy rainfall. A professional video inspection can catch cracks or root intrusion early.

## Watch for Tree Roots

Recurring slow drains, especially in older homes, can point to root intrusion. Hydro jetting or trenchless repair addresses the problem before winter makes it worse.

## Check Outdoor Fixtures

Inspect outdoor spigots for leaks and disconnect garden hoses for the season — a connected hose can trap water and contribute to freeze damage.

## Consider Preventive Drain Cleaning

A professional cleaning clears out buildup before the season's heavy rain has a chance to turn a slow drain into a backup.
