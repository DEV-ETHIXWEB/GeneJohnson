Clean water is easy to take for granted until something goes wrong. Between aging pipes and everyday waste, there's more risk to your household water supply than most homeowners realize - and more you can do about it than you might think.

## How Water Gets Contaminated

Water can pick up contaminants as it travels through pipes, particularly older or damaged ones. In serious cases, contamination has been linked to illnesses like Hepatitis A, giardia, salmonella, E. coli, and dysentery. Long-term exposure to certain contaminants, including lead, has also been connected to more serious health complications affecting the kidneys, liver, nervous system, and respiratory system.

## The Lead Pipe Risk

Homes built before 1980 are the ones most likely to still have lead piping somewhere in the system, and lead can leach into your water supply over time. If your home falls into that range and you haven't had your plumbing evaluated, it's worth having a professional take a look. Upgrading to PVC or copper piping through a [repiping](/repiping/) project removes the risk entirely.

## Protecting Your Water Supply

A whole-home water filtration system adds another layer of protection, treating water at every point it enters your house rather than at a single tap.

## Everyday Conservation Habits

Beyond safety, a few simple habits help you use water more wisely. Do a regular walkthrough of your home checking for leaks, especially around water heaters and under sinks - small leaks often go unnoticed for months. In the kitchen, keep a pitcher of water in the fridge instead of running the tap until it's cold, and try to limit the number of glasses used per day. Run the dishwasher only when it's full, and collect the water that runs while your shower warms up in a bucket to reuse for flushing the toilet.
