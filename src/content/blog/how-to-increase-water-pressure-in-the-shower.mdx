Weak shower pressure is one of those problems that's easy to live with and annoying every single day. The good news is that most causes are identifiable, and several are fixable without calling anyone.

## What's usually behind it

Aging pipes can get gunked up with mineral deposits and corrosion over the decades, majorly restricting water flow. Seattle's water conservation regulations also limit flow rates on newer fixtures by design. Beyond that, a leak somewhere in the line, mineral buildup inside the showerhead itself, or an aging water heater can all quietly rob you of pressure.

## Things worth trying yourself

**Clean the showerhead.** Soak it in equal parts white vinegar and water for about an hour, then scrub with an old toothbrush to clear out mineral deposits clogging the nozzle holes.

**Check for leaks.** Look under sinks, around the water heater, and behind shower tiles for any sign of moisture - a hidden leak elsewhere in the system can be quietly stealing pressure from your shower.

**Remove the flow restrictor.** Unscrew the showerhead and use pliers to remove the small flow restrictor disc inside, keeping in mind local water regulations before deciding this is the right move for you.

**Check your water heater settings.** Consult the manual before adjusting pressure and temperature valve settings, since this should be done carefully and within safe limits.

**Inspect the main water valve.** Make sure the valve near your water meter is fully open and free of any blockage - sometimes the fix really is that simple.

## When it's time to call a plumber

If none of the above moves the needle, the cause is probably something DIY tools can't reach - a hidden leak, corroded pipe sections, a faulty pressure regulator, or sediment buildup in the water heater that needs professional flushing equipment.
