Your thermostat is essentially the manager of your home's comfort - it reads the temperature, compares it to what you've asked for, and tells your system what to do next. When it stops doing that job correctly, your whole HVAC system suffers. Here are five signs your thermostat may be the actual problem.

## 1. The Wrong Setting Is Selected

It sounds almost too simple, but it's one of the most common culprits. Double-check that the mode is actually set to "cool" or "heat" rather than something like "fan," and that no one accidentally switched it. Sometimes the simplest explanation really is the right one.

## 2. The Switch or Lever Is Hard to Move

On older mechanical thermostats, dust and dirt can build up inside the housing and make the lever stiff or unresponsive. Regular cleaning and maintenance often resolves this before it becomes a bigger issue.

## 3. The Batteries Need Replacing

Battery-powered thermostats can fail quietly - the low-battery indicator doesn't always work as expected. It's worth putting battery replacement on a regular calendar reminder rather than waiting for a problem to appear.

## 4. Loose Internal Wiring

If connections inside the thermostat have come loose, it can misread or fail to communicate with your system entirely. This isn't a DIY fix - because it involves live electrical connections, it's best left to an HVAC technician.

## 5. Your System Works Fine Without It

If you disconnect the thermostat and your AC or furnace runs properly, that's a strong sign the thermostat itself needs troubleshooting or replacement rather than the equipment it controls.

## Consider Upgrading

If your thermostat is old or unreliable, upgrading to a programmable or smart model can pay for itself in energy savings while giving you remote control from your phone.
