Short-cycling - when a sump pump kicks on, runs briefly, and shuts off again every few minutes - isn't just annoying. It dramatically shortens the life of the pump's motor. Here's what's usually behind it.

## 1. A stuck or faulty check valve

The check valve stops water in the discharge pipe from flowing back into the pit after the pump shuts off. If it fails, that backflow re-triggers the float switch almost immediately. This is the single most common cause we find on service calls.

## 2. A float switch set too close together

If the "on" and "off" trigger points are too close, the pit refills to the "on" level almost as soon as the pump shuts off. Adjusting the float switch range - or replacing a worn switch - usually solves this.

## 3. An oversized pump for a small pit

A pump that moves more water per minute than the pit can hold will empty it almost instantly, then wait for it to refill, then empty it again. Sizing the pump to the pit (not just "bigger is better") prevents this.

## 4. Plumbing or grading sending too much water in at once

If a downspout or French drain dumps a large volume directly into the pit, even a correctly sized pump can appear to short-cycle during heavy rain. This is a drainage design issue rather than a pump problem.

## What to do about it

Short-cycling is a symptom, not a diagnosis - the fix depends entirely on which of the above is actually happening. If your pump is cycling every few minutes, it's worth having it inspected before the motor burns out entirely, especially heading into Puget Sound storm season.
