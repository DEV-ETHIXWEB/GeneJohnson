Going green doesn't have to mean a major renovation. Plenty of small, practical changes around the house cut both your environmental footprint and your utility bills - and most of them pay for themselves quickly.

## Cut Down on Landfill Waste

Bottled water is one of the easiest habits to break. About 80 percent of plastic water bottles end up in a landfill, where they take roughly 1,000 years to break down. Switching to filtered tap water with a [whole-home water filtration system](/water-filtration-systems/) gives you clean, great-tasting water on demand while cutting out plastic waste and the emissions tied to bottling and shipping.

## Shrink Your Carbon Footprint

A handful of everyday changes add up over time: biking instead of driving for short trips, switching to CFL or LED bulbs, choosing ENERGY STAR-rated appliances when it's time to replace one, turning off lights and electronics when you leave a room, buying less single-use plastic, and, for homeowners ready for a bigger step, installing solar panels.

## Upgrade Your Water Heater

Tankless water heaters use about 30 percent less energy than traditional storage tank models, since they heat water only as it's needed instead of keeping a full tank hot around the clock. They also tend to last around 20 years - roughly double the lifespan of a conventional tank. If your current [water heater](/water-heaters/) is aging, a [tankless system](/tankless-water-heaters/) is one of the highest-impact upgrades you can make.

## Save Water Without Sacrificing Comfort

A few simple habits make a real dent in water use: placing a filled water bottle in your toilet tank to reduce the volume used per flush, shutting off the water while you lather up in the shower, and setting up a rain barrel to collect water for the garden. Replacing old toilets, faucets, and showerheads with low-flow alternatives can save thousands of gallons a year without any noticeable change in your daily routine.

## Small Steps, Real Impact

None of these changes require a full remodel, but together they meaningfully reduce both your environmental impact and your monthly bills. Whether you start with a filtration system, a tankless water heater, or just a few new habits, every step counts.
