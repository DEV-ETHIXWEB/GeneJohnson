Heat pumps have come a long way since they first showed up in homes in the 1970s and 80s. Today's systems are engineered with several features specifically designed to lower energy use while improving comfort - and most homeowners never realize just how much technology is packed into the unit outside their window.

## Two-Speed Compressors

Older heat pumps run their compressor at a single speed: full blast, every time. Modern [heat pump](/heat-pump-services/) systems use two-speed compressors that automatically scale output to match actual demand, often running at around 50% capacity during milder conditions. That means less electricity used, less wear on the compressor over time, and noticeably better humidity control compared to single-speed units.

## Dual-Speed Fans and Blowers

Efficiency doesn't stop at the compressor. Dual-speed fans and blowers adjust their output the same way, which helps pull excess humidity out of the air and distribute temperature more evenly throughout the home. A side benefit many homeowners appreciate: lower fan speeds also mean a quieter system, since the blower isn't constantly running at full force.

## Smarter Supplemental Heating

Heat pumps can struggle to keep up on the coldest days, which is why older systems leaned on electric heating strips as backup - a notoriously inefficient fix. Many of today's hybrid systems instead pair the heat pump with a secondary source like natural gas, heating oil, or propane. Natural gas in particular is an economical option that also produces fewer carbon emissions than running electric resistance heat.

## Why It Matters

Together, these features mean a modern heat pump isn't the same appliance your parents may have had. It's a genuinely efficient way to heat and cool a home, especially in a moderate climate like ours here in the Seattle area.
