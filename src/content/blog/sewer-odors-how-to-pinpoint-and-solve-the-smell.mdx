A sewer smell in your home is impossible to ignore, and it usually points to something specific happening in your plumbing system rather than a mystery you have to live with. Here's how to track it down and what to do about it.

## The Most Common Causes

**Dry P-traps** are one of the most frequent culprits. When a drain goes unused for a while, the water sitting in the P-trap evaporates, allowing sewer gases to travel directly up into your home. **Clogged vents** can trap sewer gases inside the plumbing system when a roof vent gets blocked. **Damaged drain seals** - worn gaskets around a toilet or sink - let sewage smells seep into bathrooms and kitchens. **Cracked sewer pipes**, especially in older homes, release both odor and moisture as they age. **Blocked drains** trap foul smells inside the pipe itself. And outside the house, **sewer line issues** caused by tree roots, shifting soil, or a partially collapsed section can also produce a persistent odor.

## Tracking Down the Source

Work through the house room by room, checking sinks, tubs, toilets, floor drains, and laundry connections. Gurgling sounds from any of these fixtures are a useful clue - they often point to a vent problem or a blockage nearby.

## What You Can Try Yourself

Refill a dry P-trap by simply running water in that fixture for about 30 seconds. Clear a slow drain with boiling water or an enzyme-based cleaner. Tighten or replace a worn toilet seal if that's the source. Clean out the small overflow hole in a sink basin, which can trap odor-causing buildup. And clear any obstruction from a rooftop vent if you're able to safely access it.

## When to Call a Professional

If the odor persists after trying these fixes, or you notice a wet patch in the yard, gurgling from multiple drains, or an actual sewage backup, it's time to bring in a professional.
