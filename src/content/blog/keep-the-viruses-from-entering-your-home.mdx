Most homeowners think of their HVAC system purely in terms of comfort — warm in winter, cool in summer. But your heating and cooling system also plays a real role in how easily viruses spread through your home, especially during flu season.

## Temperature and Humidity Matter

Setting your thermostat too cool can dry out indoor air more than you'd expect, and dry air dries out the lining of your nose and mouth. That irritation can trigger an overproduction of mucus, which ironically makes it easier to spread germs rather than harder. Keeping humidity at a reasonable level is a small adjustment that supports your body's natural defenses instead of undermining them.

## Dirty Filters and Ducts Work Against You

Neglected air filters and ductwork don't just hurt efficiency — they can negatively affect your health. A system that's circulating dust, mold, and trapped germs is going to make cold and flu symptoms worse for everyone breathing that air, not better.

## What to Do About It

A few straightforward steps make a real difference:

- Have your ductwork and filters inspected
- Replace filters and clean ducts as needed
- Adjust your temperature settings to avoid excessively dry air
- Ask about adding a humidifier to your system

## The Bottom Line

Your home's HVAC system can do a lot more than just keep you and your family comfortable — it can also help keep you healthy. Improving your home's [air quality](/air-quality/) is one of the more overlooked ways to support a household through flu season.
