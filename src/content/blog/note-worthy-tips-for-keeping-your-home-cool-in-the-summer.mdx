Once temperatures climb into the high 70s, air conditioning stops being a luxury and starts being essential. Yet a lot of homeowners give their AC far less attention than they give their furnace every winter - usually right up until it fails on the hottest day of the year.

## Test Before You Need It

If forecasts are calling for a hot stretch, don't wait until the day it arrives to find out whether your system works. Run the unit ahead of time, confirm that every room is cooling as expected, and watch for warning signs like low coolant levels or a thermostat that isn't responding correctly. Catching a problem during a mild week gives you time to fix it - catching it during a heat wave means waiting in line with everyone else.

## Maintenance Isn't Optional

Maintenance is supposed to happen every single year, and that's especially true if the unit had a hiatus over the off-season. Skipping it doesn't save money - it just moves the cost down the road, usually attached to a bigger repair. If your unit is over 10 years old, it's also worth checking its SEER rating; anything below 11.6 is a candidate for replacement simply because newer units are so much more efficient.

## What a Technician Actually Checks

A qualified HVAC technician does more than just glance at the unit. They troubleshoot the thermostat, inspect and repair ductwork, change filters, refill refrigerant, and check the coils - all things that affect both comfort and your energy bill. High-efficiency units can cut energy consumption by 40% or more compared to an aging system, which adds up fast over a full cooling season.

## Don't Forget the Basics

Weatherstripping around windows and doors is easy to overlook, but it keeps conditioned air where it belongs instead of leaking out around the frames. It's a small, inexpensive step that meaningfully improves comfort and efficiency together.
