Granite countertops are a popular upgrade, but installing a kitchen sink into one takes more care than a typical laminate or tile setup. Here's what to think through before you start.

## Why This Isn't a DIY Job

Granite is heavy, hard, and unforgiving of mistakes. If a sink isn't properly sealed or seated, you run the risk of water leaks down the road — leaks that can damage the granite itself, the cabinetry underneath, and eventually the floor. A certified plumber knows how to seal and secure the sink correctly the first time, which matters even more with a material that's expensive to replace if something goes wrong.

## Measure Twice

Precise measurements make or break the project. You'll need the exact dimensions of the sink opening, the depth of the cabinet below, and — often overlooked — the distance to the backsplash. That last measurement affects how much clearance you'll have for a faucet and sprayer, so it's worth getting right before the granite is cut.

## Choosing the Right Sink

A few questions to work through:

- **Style** — undermount, drop-in, or a farmhouse apron sink each have different installation requirements and looks.
- **Aesthetic fit** — the sink should complement both the granite and the overall kitchen design.
- **Size and function** — think about how you actually use the kitchen. Frequent cooks may want a double-basin sink for prep and cleanup at the same time.

## Living with Granite

Granite resists heat well and holds up for decades when properly sealed, which is part of why it's such a popular countertop material. The tradeoff is during installation: cutting granite generates significant dust and requires specialized tools, which is another reason this is a job best left to professionals.
