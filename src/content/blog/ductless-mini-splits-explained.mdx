Ductless mini-split systems have gone from a niche option to one of the most requested installs we do, and for good reason - they solve a problem a huge share of Puget Sound homes actually have: no existing ductwork.

## How they work

A mini-split system has two main parts: an outdoor compressor unit and one or more indoor air-handling units, connected by a small refrigerant line set that passes through a hole just a few inches wide. Each indoor unit heats or cools its own zone independently, controlled by its own remote or a shared smart control.

## Why they've caught on here

- **No ductwork required** - a huge advantage for older homes, additions, and converted spaces like garages or bonus rooms.
- **Room-by-room control** - heat the bedroom at night without conditioning the whole house.
- **Heats and cools from one system** - modern units perform well as primary heat sources even in Puget Sound winters.
- **Quieter operation** - both indoor and outdoor units run quieter than most window AC units or older central systems.

## Is your home a good fit?

Mini-splits work well as a whole-home solution (multiple indoor heads on one or more outdoor units) or as a targeted fix for a single problem room - a converted attic, a home office addition, or a room that's always been too hot or too cold relative to the rest of the house.

The main planning question is how many zones you actually need and where the outdoor unit(s) can reasonably go.
