A furnace that fails in the middle of a cold snap is never convenient, but most furnace failures don't happen out of nowhere - they show warning signs first. Here are three of the most common problems and what to watch for.

## A Cracked Heat Exchanger

The heat exchanger is one of the most important components of a gas-powered furnace. It's the coil system responsible for distributing heated air through your home while safely containing combustion gases. Cracks can develop from a malfunction or simply from normal wear over time, and when they do, they can prevent proper heating while allowing harmful fumes to escape into your living space.

**Watch for:**
- Unusual odors
- Water leaking near the unit
- Air blowing cold or room-temperature instead of warm

A cracked exchanger usually needs to be replaced. If your furnace is already over a decade old, it's often more cost-effective to replace the whole unit rather than just the part.

## A Damaged Blower Belt

The blower fan is what actually pushes warm air through your ductwork, and its belt wears down over time. When it breaks or slips out of position, heating becomes uneven throughout the house.

**Watch for:**
- Hot and cold spots in different rooms
- A high-pitched squealing sound during operation

The good news here: this repair is typically affordable and straightforward compared to the other issues on this list.

## A Failing Starting Mechanism

Furnaces rely on either a pilot light or an electrical ignition system to start each heating cycle. When that mechanism fails, the furnace simply won't start - leaving you without heat when you need it most. A pilot light that won't stay lit often points to a failing thermocouple, which requires professional diagnosis to confirm and repair.
