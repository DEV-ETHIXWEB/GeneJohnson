A quiet plumbing system is a healthy one. So when your pipes start banging, whistling, rattling, or humming, it's worth paying attention - the noise itself isn't usually dangerous, but what's causing it sometimes is. Here are the most common culprits and what actually fixes each one.

## Water Hammer

That loud bang when a faucet or appliance shuts off suddenly is classic water hammer - a pressure shockwave moving through the pipes. Air chambers or water hammer arrestors installed near the affected fixture usually solve it; if it's happening throughout the house, your overall water pressure may need adjusting.

## Loose Pipes or Straps

Rattling or knocking, especially when water first starts flowing, often just means a pipe isn't secured tightly enough. Visible strapping can sometimes be tightened yourself; sections hidden behind walls need a plumber.

## High Water Pressure

Pressure that's too high stresses the whole system and often produces noise as a symptom. A pressure-reducing valve, or a call to your water utility, can bring things back into a safe range.

## Trapped Air in the Lines

Air pockets can cause sputtering or knocking sounds. Running the highest faucet in the house for a minute or two often clears it; if the noise keeps coming back, there may be a leak letting air back in.

## Sediment and Mineral Buildup

Years of mineral deposits inside pipes and at the water heater can cause popping or rumbling sounds, especially when the heater is running. [Hydro jetting](/hydrojetting/) clears heavy buildup that a simple flush can't touch.

## Aging Pipes

If your pipes are decades old and the noise is a symptom of broader deterioration, a full [repiping](/repiping/) may be more cost-effective than repeated spot repairs.

## Other Common Causes

Worn washers and valves, a faulty pressure regulator, loose faucet parts, water heater sediment, pipes rubbing against framing, and even a failing water meter can all produce noise with slightly different signatures.

## When to Call a Plumber

A one-time clunk after you turn off a faucet is rarely urgent. A noise that's new, getting louder, or accompanied by a drop in water pressure is worth having looked at - persistent plumbing noise left unaddressed has a way of turning into water damage.
