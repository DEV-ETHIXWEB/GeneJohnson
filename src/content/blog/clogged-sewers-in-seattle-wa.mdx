## Seattle's Trees Are Beautiful - and Hard on Sewer Lines

Take a look at a root-clogged sewer line sometime and it's genuinely amazing what tree roots can do to a pipe. Seattle's neighborhoods are full of large, mature trees, and their root systems are relentless about finding any source of moisture underground - including the small cracks and joints in a sewer line. Once roots find their way in, they grow, spread, and eventually choke off flow entirely.

## Whose Line Is It, Anyway?

It's a common point of confusion for homeowners: in most cases in Seattle, the sewer line is the homeowner's responsibility from the house all the way to the street, where it connects to the city's main line. That means root intrusion, cracks, or a full collapse on that stretch of pipe is on you to fix, not the city.

## Signs You May Have a Problem

Slow drains throughout the house, gurgling sounds, or sewage backing up into a tub or floor drain are all signs of a sewer line issue rather than an isolated clog. Because tree roots are such a common cause in this area, it's worth having your line inspected periodically, especially if you have large trees near your yard or driveway.
