Corrosion inside a [water heater](/water-heaters/) is a slow, mostly invisible process, right up until it isn't. Minerals and hard water gradually break down the protective lining inside the tank, and by the time the symptoms are obvious, the damage is often already significant. Here's what to watch for.

## Visual Warning Signs

A few signs tend to show up before a tank actually fails:

- **Discolored water** — reddish-brown discoloration or sediment when running your hot water suggests rust particles are already circulating through your pipes.
- **Tank leaks** — water pooling around the base of the tank often points to a pinhole leak caused by interior corrosion.
- **Exterior rust** — visible flaking or discoloration on the tank's outer shell is a sign corrosion has been at work for a while.
- **Unusual sounds** — rumbling, knocking, popping, or screeching sounds during heating cycles often indicate sediment and corrosion buildup inside the tank.
- **Rust at the connections** — deterioration where the inlet and outlet pipes attach to the tank is another common early sign.

## How to Check for Yourself

**Inspect the anode rod.** This sacrificial rod is designed to corrode instead of your tank. Removing and examining it periodically is one of the best ways to gauge your tank's condition — if the rod is heavily depleted, its job is essentially done, and the tank itself is now more exposed.

**Test your water quality.** An inexpensive test kit can reveal mineral hardness and iron content in your water supply. High readings mean your water itself is accelerating corrosion, regardless of how well the tank was built.

## Prevention and Solutions

Routine flushing removes sediment before it can contribute to corrosion, a water softener reduces the mineral content that drives the problem in the first place, and replacing a depleted anode rod restores an important layer of protection. In more advanced cases, full tank replacement becomes the more sensible option rather than continuing to patch a failing system.
