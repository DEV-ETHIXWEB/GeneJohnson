Winter weather has a way of finding every weak point in a home's plumbing and structure. A little preparation now can save you from dealing with a frozen pipe or a flooded basement later.

## Insulate Pipes and Faucets

Frozen and burst pipes are one of the biggest winter risks for any home. Have a plumber inspect exposed pipes for rust, corrosion, cracks, or other damage before the cold arrives. Pipes running through basements, attics, garages, and crawlspaces should be insulated with pipe foam, heating tape, or heated cables, and exterior faucets need their own foam insulation as well.

## Insulate the Pump House

If your property relies on a well, the pump house needs protection too. Installing a heat source to keep the space above freezing, along with properly insulating the pipes inside, helps prevent a frozen pump house from taking your water supply down with it.

## Check Your Foundation and Gutters

Winter precipitation can do real damage to a foundation, especially when gutters are clogged or neglected. When gutters can't do their job, water overflows and pools at the base of the foundation, which can lead to basement leaks and expensive foundation repairs. A professional inspection of both your foundation and your gutters before winter is a smart, inexpensive precaution.

## Don't Wait Until the Cold Hits

Each of these steps is far easier - and cheaper - to handle before a cold snap than after a pipe has already frozen or a foundation leak has already started.
