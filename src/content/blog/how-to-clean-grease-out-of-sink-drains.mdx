Grease is one of the sneakiest causes of a clogged kitchen sink. It goes down the drain as a warm liquid, then cools and hardens along the inside of the pipe, gradually narrowing the passage until water barely gets through. If your kitchen sink has been draining slowly, smelling off, or gurgling lately, grease buildup is a likely suspect.

## Signs You're Dealing with a Grease Clog

Slow drainage, an unpleasant odor rising from the drain, gurgling sounds in the pipes, and a clog that keeps coming back no matter how many times you clear it are all classic grease symptoms.

## Boiling Water

The simplest first step: bring a pot of water to a boil, then pour it down the drain slowly and steadily. Give it about a minute to work before running cold water to check whether it's cleared.

## Baking Soda and Vinegar

Pour about half a cup of baking soda down the drain, then follow it with half a cup of white vinegar. Cover the drain and let the mixture fizz for around 15 minutes before flushing it through with hot water. The chemical reaction helps break down grease clinging to the pipe walls.

## Salt and Hot Water

Combine half a cup of table salt with two cups of boiling water, pour it down the drain, let it sit for several minutes, then flush with cold water. Salt acts as a mild abrasive that helps loosen grease residue.

## Preventing Grease Clogs Going Forward

- Never pour grease or cooking oil down the sink - let it cool and solidify, then throw it out in the trash
- Install a grease trap if your kitchen generates a lot of cooking oil
- Run a baking soda and vinegar treatment monthly as routine maintenance

## When It's Time to Call a Plumber

If more than one drain in the house is affected, odors persist after cleaning, water is backing up unexpectedly, or none of these methods actually clear the clog, it's a sign the buildup has gone further than a sink-side fix can reach.
