Every load of laundry, shower, and load in the dishwasher sends water straight down the drain - water that, with the right system, doesn't have to go to waste. Greywater recycling is one of the more effective ways Seattle homeowners can cut water consumption without changing their daily habits.

## What Greywater Recycling Actually Does

A greywater system collects gently used water from sinks, showers, tubs, and dishwashers, purifies it, and pumps it into your toilets or outdoor spigots and taps. It isn't safe to drink - it still carries traces of soap, hair, and other residue - but it's perfectly suited for flushing toilets and watering the yard.

## Where the Reclaimed Water Goes

Homeowners commonly redirect recycled water in a couple of ways: plumbed into toilet tanks via a pumping system, or connected to outdoor faucets and irrigation setups for lawns and gardens. In the city, this typically shows up as a lower water bill. In rural areas with a septic system, it can mean less frequent tank pumping as well.

## Why Professional Installation Matters

A greywater system has to be installed correctly to keep reclaimed water completely separate from your fresh water supply. Cross-contamination is a real risk if the plumbing isn't done properly, so this is a project for a licensed plumber, not a weekend DIY job.

## Other Ways to Conserve

Greywater recycling pairs well with other simple habits: shortening showers, fixing leaks as soon as you spot them, turning off the tap while brushing your teeth, collecting rainwater, and choosing water-efficient appliances when it's time to replace one.
