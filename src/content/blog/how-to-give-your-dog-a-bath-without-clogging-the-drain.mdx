Bath time with the dog is a Seattle household ritual, but all that wet fur has to go somewhere — and too often, that "somewhere" is your drain. A little preparation goes a long way toward keeping your [drains](/drains/) clear and your plumber off speed dial.

## Prep Before the Water Runs

Give your dog a thorough brushing before you ever turn on the tap. Removing loose fur ahead of time means far less of it ends up washing down the drain in the first place. It also makes for a faster, calmer bath.

Installing a drain cover or strainer in the tub is one of the best investments you can make. These simple screens catch fur and gunk as the water drains so it never reaches your pipes at all.

## Smart Technique During the Bath

Stick to shampoo made specifically for dogs. Human formulas are too harsh for a dog's skin and tend to leave behind extra residue that adds to buildup in your pipes. Rinse thoroughly — more thoroughly than feels necessary — since leftover soap is one of the biggest contributors to slow drains over time.

If you're bathing a particularly furry breed, pause partway through to clear debris off the drain cover rather than waiting until the end. A few seconds of cleanup mid-bath prevents a bigger mess later.

## After the Bath

Once your dog is toweled off, take a minute to clear the drain properly. A drain wand or a pair of tweezers can pull out any fur that made it past the cover. Following up with a baking soda and vinegar treatment — a natural, pipe-safe combination — helps break down any residual soap film and keep things flowing.

## When It's Time to Call a Plumber

If your tub or shower starts draining slowly no matter how careful you are, the clog may be deeper in the line than a DIY fix can reach. Our team uses augers and [hydro jetting](/hydrojetting/) equipment, along with camera inspection tools, to clear stubborn blockages and check for underlying issues. Regular dog baths are worth the minor upkeep — just give your pipes the same care you give your pet.
