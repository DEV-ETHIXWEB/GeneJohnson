Your main water line is the underground pipe that carries municipal water from the street to your home's plumbing system - and because it's buried, most homeowners never think about it until something goes wrong. Here are the warning signs worth watching for.

## Water Pooling in the Yard

The most obvious red flag is water pooling up in your yard or unexplained muddy patches where there shouldn't be any. Unusually lush, green grass in one specific area can also be a sign of a slow leak feeding it. Left alone, a small leak like this tends to progress toward a full pipe burst, which turns a manageable repair into an emergency.

## Discolored Water

A break in the main line can let contaminants like dirt and heavy metals into your water supply. The key distinction is scope: if discoloration shows up throughout the house rather than at just one fixture, the main line is the likely culprit rather than a single faucet or valve.

## Reduced Water Pressure

A significant leak reduces the volume of water actually reaching your home, which shows up as noticeably lower pressure. Again, check whether the drop affects every fixture in the house or just one - that distinction helps determine whether the main line or a single component is to blame.

## Rising Water Bills

A damaged main line loses water constantly, whether you're using it or not, which shows up as a steady climb in your monthly bill. Plenty of plumbing issues can raise your water bill, but a persistent, unexplained increase is a good reason to have the main line checked.
