A burning smell from your furnace is never something to ignore. In Seattle homes, it usually points to one of a few specific causes — and figuring out which one matters, because some of them can escalate quickly.

## Motor Short Circuiting

This is the most common cause of a burning odor, and it happens when the windings on the furnace motor short circuit while the motor is still running. The smell typically resembles burning plastic, and it needs immediate professional attention — left unaddressed, this kind of short can potentially lead to a house fire.

## A Burned-Out Transformer

If you've recently upgraded your thermostat, a burned-out transformer could be the culprit. New thermostat installations can leave transformers vulnerable to short circuits, and when that happens, the resulting smell is usually described as extremely sweet rather than acrid.

## Lack of Regular Maintenance

Over time, debris — hair, dirt, pollen, dead skin, and even the occasional trapped pest — can accumulate inside the heat exchanger. When the furnace kicks on for the season, that buildup can create an odor that resembles dirty pollution rather than a true electrical smell.

## What to Do

If you notice a burning smell coming from your furnace, turn the system off immediately and call a professional before turning it back on. This isn't a symptom worth troubleshooting on your own.
