A breaker that trips once in a while is normal. One that trips constantly is your electrical panel telling you something needs attention. Here's what's usually behind it.

## Overloaded circuits

This is the most common cause: too many energy-hungry appliances, devices, and lights all trying to run on the same circuit at once. High-wattage devices like ovens, refrigerators, and air conditioning units are typical offenders, especially in older homes with fewer dedicated circuits.

## Short circuits

A more serious wiring issue where the hot and neutral wires have found an unintended connection. Short circuits can cause fires and need immediate professional attention — this isn't a DIY fix.

## Ground faults

This happens when electricity starts leaking and finds an unintentional path to ground, often caused by damaged insulation or moisture. It's exactly why modern code requires GFCI protection in kitchens and bathrooms.

## Aging electrical systems

Older wiring loses insulation integrity over time, which shows up as increasingly frequent tripping even without an obvious overload.

## Troubleshooting steps

Start by identifying which breaker is tripping and reducing the electrical load on that circuit. Attempt a reset by switching it fully off, then firmly back on. Reintroduce devices one at a time to isolate the problem. If the breaker keeps tripping despite a lighter load, that's your cue to call a professional.

## Keeping it from happening again

Schedule annual electrical inspections, balance the load across circuits rather than concentrating devices on one, and consider a [panel upgrade](/panel-replacements-upgrades/) if your system is aging or undersized for your home's current usage.

If tripping keeps happening no matter what you unplug, [contact us](/contact/) — persistent tripping is often a symptom of a bigger issue in the panel or wiring.
