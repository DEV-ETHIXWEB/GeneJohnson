Water carries minerals, dirt, and sand no matter where it comes from, and over time that sediment settles at the bottom of your water heater tank. As it builds up, your water heater has to work harder to heat the same amount of water - which means higher energy bills and, eventually, a shorter lifespan for the unit.

## Why Draining Matters

Periodically draining your water heater flushes out that accumulated sediment, letting the unit run the way it was designed to. Skipping this maintenance step lets the buildup keep growing, quietly working against your energy bills the whole time.

## Why This Isn't a DIY Job

It's easy to make a mess and just as easy to burn yourself in the process, so this is a job best left to someone who does it regularly. A professional will turn off power to the unit, shut the cold water supply valve, attach a drain hose to the drain valve, and flush the remaining water out through a faucet - collecting some of the runoff to check how much sediment has actually built up.

## When to Flush Again

If that sample comes back with a noticeable amount of sediment, it may be necessary to refill and drain the tank a second time to get it fully clean.
