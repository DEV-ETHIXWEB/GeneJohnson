You don't need a major renovation to cut down your water bill - a handful of small habits and a bit of maintenance awareness can add up to real savings over a year.

## Choose showers over baths

Taking baths requires a lot more water than most people realize. The amount of water needed to fill a bathtub is usually about three or four times more than a typical shower uses. Shortening your shower time and turning off the water while brushing your teeth are two of the simplest changes you can make.

## Fix leaks quickly

A leak that seems minor can waste hundreds of gallons of water in a month. That includes a running toilet, which is one of the most common - and most overlooked - sources of wasted water in a home. If something's dripping or running longer than it should, get it repaired promptly rather than letting it become a habit you tune out.

## Reuse greywater

Gently used water from sinks, tubs, and washing machines can be repurposed to water plants and trees instead of going straight down the drain. It's worth checking with a local nursery about regulations in your area and which plants tolerate greywater well before setting up a system.

## Skip the home car wash

Washing your car at home takes a lot longer than the time needed at a commercial car wash, and typically uses more water overall. Using a commercial facility is often the more water-efficient choice, even accounting for the drive there.
