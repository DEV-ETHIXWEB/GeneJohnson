Seattle's rainy, mild climate is great for a lot of things. Keeping bathrooms dry isn't one of them. Between shower steam, high ambient humidity, and drains that trap moisture around fixtures, our bathrooms give mold exactly what it needs to take hold - especially in grout lines and around faucets where condensation lingers.

## Make ventilation a habit, not an afterthought

The single biggest thing you can do is get moist air out of the room. Run your exhaust fan for at least 20 minutes after every shower, and crack a window or door when you can to keep air moving. If your bathroom doesn't have an exhaust fan, or the one it has can't keep up, that's worth fixing before mold becomes a recurring problem.

## Wipe down what stays wet

Mold needs standing moisture to grow, so don't give it any. Wipe down tile, shower doors, and countertops right after bathing, and get damp towels and bath mats out of the room instead of leaving them bunched on the floor.

## Stay on top of cleaning

- Wash shower curtains regularly with a bit of bleach, and swap plastic liners every few months.
- Deep-clean grout every few months with a dedicated cleaner or a baking soda and vinegar paste.
- Replace cracked or peeling caulk with a fresh, mold-resistant silicone sealant.
- Check under sinks and around the tub periodically for slow leaks - a hidden drip is a mold colony waiting to happen.

## Consider longer-term upgrades

If mold keeps coming back no matter how diligent you are, a dehumidifier or an upgraded exhaust fan system can permanently shift the humidity balance in the room. Replacing worn grout and caulk, and choosing mold-resistant materials during any bathroom remodel, pays off for years.

## When it's time to call a plumber

Musty odors and discolored patches are your cue to act, not wait. If mold keeps returning near a specific fixture, it's often a sign of a slow leak inside the wall or under the floor rather than just ambient humidity. That's not a job for bleach and a sponge - it calls for finding and fixing the source.
