Water conservation doesn't get talked about as much as other environmental priorities, but it's one of the easier ones to actually do something about. The EPA estimates that the average American family uses around 400 gallons of water per day - and a surprising amount of that is waste hiding in plain sight.

## Start with a plumbing audit

Having a local plumber inspect your pipes, fixtures, and drains is the most direct way to find out where water is being lost. Leaks aren't always obvious - sometimes they're just a few gallons a day disappearing somewhere behind a wall or under a slab.

## Catching what you can't hear

Professionals use listening devices and acoustic amplifiers that can detect leaks too faint for the human ear, along with fiber-optic video cameras that let us inspect the inside of pipes on a monitor. Catching a slow leak this way, before it becomes obvious from water damage or a spiked bill, saves both water and money.

## Don't overlook your toilets

A running toilet is one of the most common sources of wasted water in a home, often from nothing more than a worn flapper. Replacing that flapper - or upgrading to a modern dual-flush toilet that lets you choose the right amount of water for the job - is a small fix with an outsized impact on your monthly usage.

## Small upgrades add up

Beyond leaks and toilets, a plumber can point out which fixtures and appliances are working against you - older showerheads, faucets without aerators, or an aging water heater running less efficiently than it should. None of these fixes are dramatic on their own, but together they add up to a meaningfully lower water bill and a lighter footprint.

## Make it a yearly habit

Earth Day is as good a prompt as any to schedule a plumbing checkup, but water conservation works best as an ongoing habit rather than a once-a-year event.
