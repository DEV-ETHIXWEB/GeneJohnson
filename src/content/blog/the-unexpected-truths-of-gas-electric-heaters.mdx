Choosing between a gas and an electric heating system usually comes down to a handful of tradeoffs that aren't always obvious until you dig into how each one actually works.

## How Ducted Heating Works

Most home heating systems, gas or electric, distribute warm air through ducts to vents in the ceiling or floor. Gas ducted heating is a common and economical choice in colder climates, while electric-only systems skip gas entirely and rely on electricity from start to finish. Split systems, by contrast, run on a gas- or oil-fueled furnace.

## Gas vs. Electric: Cost and Speed

Gas heating is generally less expensive to run and heats a home faster than electric heat. That said, gas furnaces cost more upfront because of the venting required for safe installation. Electric furnaces flip that equation - lower installation cost, but typically higher operating costs over time, and they tend to make more sense in milder climates or areas without natural gas service.

## The Efficiency Question

It's worth knowing that electricity is generated through processes that are only about thirty percent efficient by the time it reaches your home. Natural gas isn't emission-free either, but it burns cleaner than coal, which means less impact on the environment per unit of heat produced.

## Where Heat Pumps Fit In

If you want the best of both worlds, a [heat pump](/heat-pump-services/) is worth a look. It cools your home in summer and heats it in winter, all with significantly better energy efficiency than a traditional gas or electric furnace running heating duty alone.

## Making the Right Call

There's no universal right answer here - it depends on your home, your climate, and your budget.
