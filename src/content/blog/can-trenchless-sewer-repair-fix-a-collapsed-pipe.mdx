A collapsed sewer line sounds like a guaranteed excavation project, and for decades it was. Today, trenchless repair can fix many collapsed sections through one or two small access pits instead of a trench running the length of your yard.

## How trenchless repair actually works

There are two main trenchless methods:

- **Pipe bursting** - a new pipe is pulled through the old one, literally bursting the failed pipe outward as it goes, leaving a new structural pipe in its place.
- **Cured-in-place lining (CIPP)** - a resin-saturated liner is fed into the existing pipe and cured in place, forming a new pipe within the old one.

Both methods avoid tearing up landscaping, driveways, and hardscape that a traditional dig would destroy.

## Not every collapse qualifies

Trenchless repair isn't universal. A fully collapsed section with no remaining pipe shape usually can't be lined - pipe bursting is the more likely candidate there, since it doesn't rely on the old pipe holding a shape. Severely offset joints, bellies (low spots that hold water), and certain pipe materials can also rule out lining specifically.

This is exactly why we always start with a **camera inspection** before quoting any sewer repair. It's the only way to see the actual condition of the pipe rather than guessing based on symptoms at the surface.

## Warning signs worth a camera inspection

- Recurring backups in the same drain
- Gurgling sounds from toilets or drains
- A sudden soft spot or sinkhole in the yard
- Sewage odor near a cleanout or in the yard

If you're seeing any of these, it's worth scheduling a [sewer camera inspection](/drain-camera-scoping/) before the problem gets worse - and before you assume a full yard excavation is your only option.
