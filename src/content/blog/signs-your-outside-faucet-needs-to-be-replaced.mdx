Outdoor faucets take a beating from the elements year-round, and eventually that wear catches up with them. Here are the signs we look for when deciding whether an outdoor [faucet](/faucets-fixtures-sinks/) needs to be replaced rather than repaired.

## Constant Dripping

That incessant drip, drip, dripping from your outdoor faucet is more than just an annoyance — it signals worn internal components, and it wastes water and adds to your utility bill every single day it continues.

## Leaking Around the Handle

Moisture around the handle stem points to failed seals inside the faucet. Left unaddressed, that moisture can lead to corrosion and even attract pests looking for a water source.

## Reduced Water Pressure

Weak water pressure is one of the clearest indicators that this hard-working fixture is ready for replacement, usually caused by mineral deposits building up and restricting flow inside the faucet body.

## Rust and Corrosion

Constant exposure to the elements causes discoloration and gradual deterioration, which affects both how the faucet looks and how well it actually functions.

## Stiff Handles

Years of mineral buildup, internal wear, or even freeze damage can make turning those handles maddeningly stiff — and a handle that's hard to operate can also become a safety hazard if it needs to be shut off quickly.

## Freezing in Winter

A faucet that freezes is at real risk of cracking, which can lead to a burst pipe and water damage once things thaw.

## Water Pooling at the Base

Puddles collecting at the base of the faucet point to failed internal seals that a simple washer replacement usually won't fix.

## Unusual Noises

Groaning or screeching sounds when the faucet is running usually mean mineral buildup is restricting water flow inside.

## Visible Cracks

Any crack in the faucet body can escalate into a burst pipe and significant water damage, especially once temperatures drop.

## Consider Its Age

If your outdoor faucet is 10 to 15 years old, it's worth considering proactive replacement rather than waiting for one of the issues above to force the decision.
