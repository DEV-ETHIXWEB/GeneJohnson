Your electrical panel is the gatekeeper for every watt of power that flows through your house. It was almost certainly sized for the home as it existed when it was built — not for the home you're living in now, with more devices, more appliances, and more demand than ever. Here's how to recognize when your panel has fallen behind.

## Your Lifestyle Has Outgrown Your Panel's Capacity

The clearest sign isn't a single symptom — it's a pattern. If you've added a home office, a workshop, a hot tub, or are considering an [EV charger](/electric-vehicle-chargers/), your electrical demand has grown even if your panel hasn't. Older panels simply weren't built with today's appliance lineup in mind.

## Breakers Trip More Than They Used To

Occasional trips during a storm are normal. Breakers that trip regularly during everyday use — running the dryer and the microwave, say — mean the panel is being asked to do more than it's rated for.

## Lights Flicker or Dim Under Load

If lights dim noticeably when a large appliance kicks on, your panel isn't distributing power evenly or consistently, which is a capacity problem more than a fixture problem.

## You've Renovated or Expanded Your Home

Added square footage means added circuits. A panel that was appropriately sized for a 1,500-square-foot house isn't necessarily sized for the same house after a 400-square-foot addition.

## Buzzing, Burning Smells, or Scorched Outlets

These are not "keep an eye on it" signs — they're emergency signs. A burning smell or visibly scorched outlet near the panel warrants an immediate call to a licensed electrician.

## Why It's Worth Addressing Now

Beyond the fire risk, an undersized panel limits what you can plug in, drives up energy costs through inefficiency, and can complicate a home sale. Seattle's humid climate doesn't do aging panels any favors either — moisture accelerates the kind of wear that turns a minor issue into a serious one.
