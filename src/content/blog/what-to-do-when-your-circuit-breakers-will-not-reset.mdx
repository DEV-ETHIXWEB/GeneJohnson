A breaker that won't reset — no matter how many times you flip it — is more than an annoyance. It usually means something is actively preventing the circuit from safely carrying power, and it's worth understanding why before you keep trying to force it.

## Common causes

**Overloaded circuit.** The most frequent culprit: too many high-demand appliances running on the same circuit at once. Space heaters, hair dryers, and kitchen appliances are common offenders.

**Electrical short.** A more serious condition where live and neutral wires make direct contact, causing a power surge that trips the breaker as a protective measure.

**Ground fault.** Moisture or water exposure can trigger the same kind of safety shutdown you'd see from a GFCI outlet — common in bathrooms, kitchens, and outdoor circuits.

**GFCI/AFCI protection doing its job.** These built-in safety features cut power the moment they detect a hazardous fault or electrical arcing, which is exactly what they're designed to do.

## How to troubleshoot it

Start by identifying exactly which breaker is tripped, then unplug everything on that circuit. Reset carefully by flipping the breaker fully to off, then firmly back to on. Inspect the panel and outlets for any visible signs of damage. If it still won't hold, that's your signal to call a professional rather than keep resetting it.

## Preventing it going forward

Avoid clustering power-hungry devices on a single circuit, use surge protectors sensibly, and schedule regular [electrical inspections](/electrical-safety-inspections/) — especially if your panel is older. Persistent tripping can indicate deteriorating wiring or a panel that's no longer sized for your home's actual power needs, both of which need expert diagnosis.
