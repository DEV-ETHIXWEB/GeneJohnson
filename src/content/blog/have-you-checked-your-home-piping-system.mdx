Preventing a burst pipe is always better — and cheaper — than cleaning up after one. Seattle winters don't always feel severe, but it doesn't take much of a cold snap to put unprotected pipes at risk, and the cost of repairing a burst pipe (plus any water damage that follows) is far greater than the cost of a little prevention now.

## Insulate Exposed Pipes

Any pipe exposed to the outdoors or to unheated spaces becomes vulnerable once temperatures drop below freezing. Water expands as it freezes, and that expansion is what cracks pipes — a crack that often doesn't reveal itself as a leak until the ice thaws. Professional foam pipe insulation is a simple, inexpensive way to protect exposed lines through the cold months.

## Shut Off and Drain Outdoor Taps

Before winter sets in, disconnect hoses from your outdoor spigots and shut the interior valve that feeds them, while leaving the exterior valve open. This allows any remaining water in the line to drain out rather than sitting there and freezing solid.

## Have a Plumber Inspect Your Supply Lines

A qualified plumber can check the drainage line between your shut-off and exterior valves to confirm it's draining properly. If water isn't draining as it should, the line can freeze and split without much warning.

## An Ounce of Prevention

The cost of repairing — or in some cases redoing — an entire section of pipe work is far greater than the cost of insulation, valve maintenance, and a seasonal inspection. If you haven't had your home's plumbing checked before winter, now is the time.
