Commercial plumbing systems take a different kind of beating than residential ones - higher traffic, more users, and less patience for downtime. These are the four issues we run into most often in Seattle-area commercial buildings, and how we address each one.

## Leaking pipes

Leaking pipes are very common in commercial settings, and they can go undetected for weeks or even months in a large building. Watch for reduced water pressure, an unexplained jump in the water bill, or visible moisture near walls and fixtures. Causes range from ordinary corrosion to excessive water pressure stressing the system. We use professional [leak detection](/water-leak-detection/) to pinpoint the source and determine whether a targeted repair or a section replacement is the right call.

## Clogged drains

High-traffic facilities see drain blockages far more often than homes do, frequently from paper towels, hygiene products, or other items that shouldn't go down a commercial drain in the first place. [Hydro jetting](/hydrojetting/) - using pressurized water to fully clear the interior of the pipe - is our go-to fix here, since it clears buildup completely rather than just punching through it temporarily.

## Clogged toilets

Heavy daily use can overwhelm commercial toilet systems faster than a standard plunger can keep up with. When basic methods don't clear it, our technicians use specialized snake tools designed to dislodge tougher blockages without damaging commercial fixtures.

## Sewer backups

An obstructed sewer line can back sewage up into the building itself - a serious problem for any business. We start with a camera inspection to locate the blockage, then use hydro jetting or [trenchless repair](/trenchless-sewer-line-repair-replacement/) technology, which clears debris while reinforcing the pipe, minimizing disruption to your operation.

## Why it pays to stay ahead of these

Commercial plumbing problems tend to escalate faster than residential ones simply because more people are using the same fixtures every day. Regular maintenance and prompt attention to early symptoms keep small issues from turning into a shutdown.
