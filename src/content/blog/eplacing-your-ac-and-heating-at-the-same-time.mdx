When one HVAC system starts failing, it raises an obvious question: should you replace it alone, or take the opportunity to replace your heating and cooling systems together?

## Start With a Professional Assessment

Before deciding either way, it's worth having a technician evaluate the expected lifespan of your current equipment. That assessment, combined with a realistic forecast of upcoming repair costs, gives you the information you actually need to make the call rather than guessing.

## The Case for Replacing Both Together

**Energy efficiency.** Newer systems use significantly less energy than older ones, which can lower your utility bills starting in the very first year — and it's better for the environment, too.

**One installation instead of two.** Combining the projects means you pay for a single installation visit instead of scheduling and paying for two separate jobs down the road.

**Matched systems work better together.** A [cooling](/cooling/ac-installation/) system and heating system installed together are designed to operate efficiently as a pair, share compatible components, and need fewer repairs. Mixing old and new technology can create performance conflicts that show up as higher bills or uneven comfort.

## The Tradeoff: Upfront Cost

The obvious downside is the size of the initial investment. If one of your two systems still has years of useful life left, it may make more sense to replace only the failing one now and plan for the other later.

## Talk It Through With a Professional

There's no single right answer for every home. A qualified HVAC technician can help you weigh the upfront cost against the long-term efficiency and installation savings to figure out what makes sense for your situation. [Schedule now](/schedule-now/) for an assessment.
