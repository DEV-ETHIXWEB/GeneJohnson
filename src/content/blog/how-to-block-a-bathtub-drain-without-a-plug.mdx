Lost your bathtub plug and need a bath tonight? A few household items can get you a workable seal until you can pick up a proper replacement.

## Temporary options that actually work

**A washcloth or sponge.** Dampen the material, roll it tightly, and firmly press and twist it down into the drain opening, compacting it to create a tight seal.

**A water-filled plastic bag.** Fill a ziplock bag about three-quarters full of water, seal it, and lay it over the drain opening. Double-bagging adds extra insurance against leaks.

**A jar lid.** Find a plastic lid slightly larger than the drain opening, then press down firmly on the center to create an air-tight seal via suction.

**An inflated balloon.** Tie it off securely and push it into the drain to create a pressure-based block.

**A silicone baking liner.** These don't absorb water, which makes them a surprisingly reliable temporary stopper when pressed firmly over the drain.

**A drain cover or strainer.** If you have one lying around, it can work in a pinch - weigh it down if it doesn't sit flush.

## Don't rely on these long-term

These are stopgaps, not solutions. Relying on improvised stoppers for more than a bath or two risks nasty clogs, mold issues, or even cracked pipes from built-up pressure in ways they weren't designed to handle.

## Get a real replacement

Universal stoppers, suction plugs, and suction cup drain covers are inexpensive and solve the problem permanently.
