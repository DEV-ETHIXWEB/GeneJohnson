Plumbing advice gets passed around like folklore — a tip from a neighbor, a trick from an old home-improvement article — and not all of it holds up. Here are three of the most common myths we hear from homeowners, and what actually works instead.

## Myth: Lemon Rinds Clean Your Garbage Disposal

It's a popular trick, but lemon rinds don't actually disinfect a [garbage disposal](/garbage-disposals/) — they just make it smell nice for a few minutes while doing nothing about the buildup underneath. For an actual clean, unplug the disposal first for safety, then let warm soapy water soak in the unit for about 15 minutes before scrubbing with a brush. That combination cuts through grime that citrus alone never touches.

## Myth: Hot Water Prevents Clogs

Running hot water down the [drain](/drains/) feels like it should flush everything away, but it won't save you if you're regularly pouring grease and oil down the sink — those substances cool and re-solidify further down the pipe regardless of how hot the water was going in. The same goes for fibrous scraps like celery, and items like cigarettes or banana peels that don't grind easily in a disposal. Hot water is not a substitute for being careful about what goes down the drain in the first place.

## Myth: Pipes and Fixtures Don't Need Attention Until Something Breaks

Plumbing infrastructure benefits from regular inspection, not just emergency response. Check exposed pipes periodically for leaks or corrosion, and clean fixtures regularly — but skip the hand soap. It might seem like a convenient cleaner, but it tends to leave finishes dull and dirty rather than shiny.
