Seattle's fall and winter months put more pressure on your home's drains than any other time of year. Between falling leaves, heavier cooking, and freezing temperatures, it's the season when small plumbing issues are most likely to turn into real problems.

## What Changes This Time of Year

Fall brings a wave of debris and a jump in cooking activity, while winter adds the risk of freezing. Grease and oils from holiday cooking solidify faster in cooler temperatures, making kitchen drains more prone to clogging. Outdoor drains back up as leaves pile onto grates. Exposed pipes are at real risk of freezing and, in the worst cases, bursting. And bathroom drains see more traffic as families spend more time indoors.

## Preventive Steps Worth Taking

Clear gutters and outdoor drains regularly through the fall so leaves don't have a chance to compact into a blockage. Avoid pouring grease down the kitchen sink, no matter how tempting it is after a big meal - let it cool and toss it instead. Install drain screens to catch food scraps and debris before they enter the pipe, and run hot water after using the sink to help keep things moving. Any exposed pipes, especially in a crawlspace, garage, or exterior wall, should be insulated before the first hard freeze.

## Why Professional Checkups Help

Professional plumbers use specialized tools to clear blockages thoroughly and can spot hidden issues - a partially cracked pipe, a slow leak, aging seals - before they turn into an emergency. Think of a seasonal plumbing checkup the same way you'd think of servicing your car before a road trip: cheap insurance against a much bigger bill.

## Signs You Shouldn't Ignore

Slow drainage, gurgling sounds, foul odors, pooling water, or a clog that keeps coming back are all signals that it's time to call in help rather than wait it out. Catching these early, before the coldest months set in, can save you from dealing with a frozen or burst pipe in the middle of a January cold snap.
