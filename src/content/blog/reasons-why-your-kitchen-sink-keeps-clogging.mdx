If your kitchen sink keeps backing up no matter how many times you clear it, something upstream of the drain is the real problem. Here's what's usually behind it, and what actually fixes it.

## Common Causes

- **Food waste and grease** — seafood scraps and fatty residue don't just wash away; they solidify inside the pipe over time and build into a blockage.
- **Soap buildup** — dish soap residue coats the inside of pipes, gradually narrowing how much water can pass through.
- **Foreign objects** — a dropped utensil or small container can catch debris and turn into the start of a clog.
- **Mineral deposits** — depending on your local water supply, calcium and magnesium can build up inside pipes and restrict flow over months or years.

## DIY Steps Worth Trying

- Compost or trash food scraps instead of rinsing them down the drain.
- For routine maintenance, mix half a cup of baking soda with a cup of vinegar, let it fizz for about 15 minutes, then flush it with a kettle of boiling water.
- Install a mesh drain strainer to catch particles before they ever make it into the pipe.
- Use an enzyme-based cleaner periodically to help break down buildup between deeper cleanings.

## When to Call a Plumber

Some signs mean it's past the point of a DIY fix: drainage that stays slow no matter what you try, gurgling sounds coming from the drain, water backing up in other fixtures at the same time, or any indication of a pipe under real strain. At that point, professional options like [hydrojetting](/hydrojetting/) — which clears buildup with high-pressure water — or drain snaking with an auger cable can clear blockages that DIY methods can't touch.

## Keeping It From Coming Back

Educate everyone in the household about what shouldn't go down the sink, schedule an annual professional [drains](/drains/) inspection, and if you're in a hard water area, a water softening system can cut down on mineral buildup throughout your plumbing, not just at the kitchen sink.
