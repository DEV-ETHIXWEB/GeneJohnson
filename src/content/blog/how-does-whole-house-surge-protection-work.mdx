Power surges happen more often than most homeowners realize, and not every one comes from a dramatic lightning strike. A power surge is simply a brief spike in the electrical voltage flowing through your home's wiring and appliances, and it can originate from a lightning strike, a utility company fluctuation, or even something as ordinary as a refrigerator or air conditioner cycling on and off. [Whole-house surge protection](/surge-protection/) is designed to guard against all of it at once, rather than protecting just one outlet at a time.

## How the System Works

A whole-house system is built around a surge protection device (SPD) that's hardwired directly into your main electrical panel. The SPD continuously monitors the electrical flow into your home, and the moment it detects a voltage spike, it redirects that excess voltage and diffuses it harmlessly into the ground through your home's grounding system, before it ever reaches your outlets or appliances.

## What's Inside an SPD

A quality surge protection device includes specialized monitoring circuitry to detect abnormal voltage, heavy-gauge wiring connecting it to the panel, a solid grounding system to disperse the excess voltage safely, and built-in safety features like a clamping voltage rating and thermal protection to keep the device itself from becoming a hazard.

## Why Professional Installation Matters

Because the device is wired directly into your main panel, installation should always be handled by a licensed electrician. Improper setup can create a genuine safety hazard rather than the protection it's meant to provide.

## What to Look for When Choosing One

Verify that any system you're considering carries UL certification, check its surge current rating (a minimum of 40,000 amps is generally recommended), and make sure it's sized to cover all the circuits in your home, not just a portion of them.

## More Reliable Than Plug-In Strips

A whole-house system provides continuous, round-the-clock protection for everything connected to your electrical system, which is a meaningfully higher level of security than a plug-in power strip protecting a single device.
