Your electrical panel is the command center for your home's entire electrical system — every circuit in the house traces back to it. When it's working properly, you never think about it. When it's outdated, it quietly becomes one of the biggest fire and safety risks in your home. Here's how to tell which category yours falls into.

## Breakers That Trip Constantly

If a breaker trips every time you run the microwave and the air conditioner at once, that's not just an inconvenience — it's your panel telling you it doesn't have the capacity for how your home actually uses electricity today.

## Fuses Instead of Breakers

If you're still opening a fuse box rather than a breaker panel, you're working with technology that predates most of the safety improvements in modern electrical codes. Fuses have to be physically replaced after every trip and offer far less protection than a modern breaker.

## Too Little Amperage

Many older homes in the Seattle area were wired for 60 to 100 amps of service. Modern households — with more appliances, electronics, and increasingly EV chargers — typically need 200 amps to run comfortably and safely.

## Visible Rust, Corrosion, or Damage

Our humid climate isn't kind to aging metal panels. Rust or corrosion on the panel housing is a sign it's nearing the end of its useful life and should be inspected right away.

## Burning Smells or Heat Coming from the Panel

This is not a wait-and-see situation. Any burning odor or noticeable heat near the panel means you should shut off power to it, if you can safely do so, and call a licensed electrician immediately.

## No Room to Grow

If your panel is already full and you can't add a circuit for a new appliance, addition, or [EV charger](/electric-vehicle-chargers/), it's undersized for your home's future, not just its present.

## A History of Recalls

Certain older panel brands have well-documented safety problems and recall histories. A qualified electrician can tell you quickly whether yours is one of them.

Electrical distribution issues are tied to hundreds of fires each year, and an outdated panel is a common thread. If any of these signs sound familiar, schedule a professional [electrical safety inspection](/electrical-safety-inspections/) rather than waiting for a bigger warning sign to show up.
