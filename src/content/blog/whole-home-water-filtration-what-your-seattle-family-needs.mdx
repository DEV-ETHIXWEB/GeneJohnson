Seattle's water starts out clean, drawn from protected watersheds that are the envy of many cities. But by the time it reaches your tap, it's traveled through miles of municipal piping — and that journey can pick up more than you'd expect.

## Why Seattle Homes Still Need Filtration

Homeowners across the area commonly report a chlorine taste or odor in their drinking water, dry and itchy skin after showering, mineral deposits building up on dishes, sediment clogging faucet aerators, and appliances wearing out faster than they should due to mineral accumulation. None of these are signs of unsafe water, exactly — but they're signs that a [whole-home water filtration system](/water-filtration-systems/) could make a real difference.

## How Whole-Home Systems Work

Unlike a pitcher filter or a faucet attachment that treats water at one location, a whole-home system installs at the main water line where it enters your house, filtering everything before it's distributed to a single tap, shower, or appliance. A typical setup includes sediment filtration to remove debris, a carbon filter to reduce chlorine taste and odor, and optional specialty filters for specific concerns like dissolved metals.

## What Homeowners Notice

Most families notice a difference almost immediately — better-tasting water, softer skin and hair after showering, clearer laundry, less scale on fixtures, and appliances that run more efficiently and last longer without mineral buildup working against them.

## Whole-Home vs. Point-of-Use

The key advantage of a whole-home system is coverage. It treats water at every outlet in the house — showers, washing machines, outdoor spigots, kitchen taps — rather than just the one faucet a pitcher or countertop filter can reach.
